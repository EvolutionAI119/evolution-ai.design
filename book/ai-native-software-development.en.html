Chapter 1 Defining AI-Native Software and Its Boundaries

Author: Quantum Swordsman Volume I · The Case for AI-Native

1. The Problem: "AI Features Added" Is Not AI-Native

A supplier's styling department added an "AI generate" button to its existing CAD pipeline. A designer typed "low stance, long wheelbase, sporty SUV," the model returned three renderings, and the review meeting applauded. Three months later the project entered engineering and the failures arrived together. Results could not be reproduced: the same prompt produced a different car on a different machine. The model occasionally failed, yet the interface still said "generation succeeded," and engineers carried empty data downstream. Worst of all was acceptance: there was no criterion for whether a rendering was good, so reviews were settled by volume.

This is not a model-capability problem. It is a software-form problem. The team "added an AI feature to a legacy system" rather than "built an AI-native system." The difference is not whether a model is invoked. The difference is whether the system admits that models err, go missing, and drift—and whether those uncertainties occupy first-class positions in the architecture, with types, states, and exits. This chapter defines AI-native software, states four principles, and draws four negative boundaries.

2. Principles

Principle 1: Uncertainty enters the architecture. The first criterion of AI-native is not "has a model" but that uncertainty is modeled as observable objects: input distributions, confidence intervals, failure modes, and degradation paths each have a place. The test is plain: open the architecture diagram and look for the line labeled "model unavailable." If it is absent, the system is not AI-native; it has merely mistaken luck for design.

Principle 2: Evaluation precedes generation. Generation can be procured; evaluation must be built in-house. Generation without an evaluation function is a lottery; generation with one is a search. Evaluation must also be regression-safe: the same input under the same rubric yields the same score, otherwise the score is rhetoric and has no standing in review.

When this does not apply: during divergent exploration—when no objective function exists yet—evaluation yields to diversity sampling. Map the shape of the space first; calibrate the ruler afterward.

Principle 3: Capability boundaries are explicit. Signal, never simulate. When a dependency is missing, return "unavailable" rather than filling the interface with fabricated data. Degradation must be labeled, and degraded results must not enter the decision chain. Honesty is an engineering requirement, not a moral posture: once a fabricated number pollutes the decision chain, every downstream step pays interest on it.

Principle 4: Judgment stays with people. AI-native does not mean unmanned. The machine supplies candidates, evidence, and advice; people supply value judgment and accountability. The terminal state of the system should be an auditable "decided by a person," not a stretch of model output—to hand over judgment is to hand responsibility to an entity that cannot answer for it.

Four boundaries follow: AI-native is not "features plus AI," not "prompt as architecture," not "model as truth," and not "no humans." All four say the same thing—uncertainty is acknowledged, located, and handled.

3. Scope and Boundaries

The methodology in this book is not a universal prescription. It is engineering experience distilled from one specific class of system: AI-native systems in which evaluation is expensive, hard engineering constraints exist, and deliverables must be auditable. What these systems share: every generation carries a priceable quality criterion (a continuity assessment, a prototype trial, a regression suite), deliverables are reviewed by third parties (regulators, customers, downstream factories), and errors have real costs. Every principle in this book—evaluation before generation, explicit boundaries, regression-safe contracts—is written for this class.

Three kinds of systems fall outside the framework's direct applicability; individual principles may still be borrowed:

  • Purely exploratory products. Creative tools without explicit acceptance criteria (open-ended writing, brainstorming boards) aim at divergence, not convergence; "evaluation before generation" should yield to diversity sampling. What transfers: making uncertainty explicit and degrading honestly.
  • Hyperscale GPU training platforms. Scheduling, fault tolerance, and throughput optimization for thousand-card clusters form a discipline of their own, beyond the reach of the layering and kernel principles here. What transfers: contract-first design and layered testing.
  • One-off scripts. Disposable data-processing scripts end at delivery and have no interface surface worth defending. What transfers: the habit of "parameters as the single source of truth," not the full architecture.

Drawing the boundary is not weakness; it is the methodology's honesty. A principle that cannot state its conditions of applicability is only a slogan.

4. Case Anatomy: How EVOLUTION AI Gives the Four Principles Structure

EVOLUTION AI is an automotive styling platform. It qualifies as an AI-native specimen not because it uses large models, but because each principle has a checkable structural counterpart.

Input is constrained into parameters. A vehicle is described by the 22-dimensional CarParams (algorithm_model/car_modeling/car_params.py), covering primary dimensions, segment lengths, ground clearance, styling angles and arcs, glass depth, and wheel and lamp details. Only 14 dimensions enter Bayesian search (backend/app/bayes_optimizer.py); the other eight are frozen as regulatory, structural, and dimensional hard points. Uncertainty is not eliminated; it is confined to a bounded room.

Evaluation landed before generation. Quality evaluation emits G0/G1/G2 counts, reflection-line scores, and an A–D composite grade, with fixed tolerances of 0.1 mm / 1.0° and thresholds of 5° for G1 and 2° for G2. The division of labor between the two threshold sets is given in the dual-link quality threshold table in Chapter 5. These numbers encode surface-engineering consensus; they did not grow out of the model. Consequently the same parameter version reproduces the same quality report, and the score earns its place in review.

Boundaries are explicit. When PyTorch is missing, training endpoints fail loudly instead of returning fake progress. When CLIP weights are absent, texture analysis falls back to geometric features and says so in the response. When Redis is absent, session storage degrades to in-process memory. When no LLM key exists, chat requests return an explicit error rather than a fabricated answer. Capabilities vary in strength; boundaries do not blur.

Acceptance is institutionalized. The roughly 1,027 automated tests (200 algorithm, 411 backend, 416 frontend—as of 2026-10-25, per docs/VALIDATION_REPORT_20261025.md) are a regression baseline, not coverage decoration: the contracts of 19 route modules and 154 HTTP endpoints (152 routed plus 2 system), the ownership of 17 ORM tables, and the responsibilities of 12 frontend pages are all pinned by tests and documentation together. Trust comes from repeatable evidence, not from one brilliant demo.

Cross-domain mapping. These principles do not presuppose geometry; they hold unchanged in other domains:

  • In this platform = a missing dependency returns "unavailable" with degradation labeled (training endpoints fail explicitly without PyTorch; texture analysis falls back to geometric features and says so without CLIP); in a RAG system = when retrieval finds no basis, the system declares "I don't know" instead of fabricating an answer. Same principle: uncertainty enters the architecture and boundaries are explicit.
  • In this platform = the same parameter version reproduces the same quality report under fixed tolerances (0.1 mm / 1.0°); in LLM evaluation = same input, same score, with baselines locked and drift alarms. Same requirement: evaluation precedes generation, and evaluation is regression-safe.

5. Antipatterns and Their Cost

AI as a decorative layer. A generation entry is bolted onto a side path of the legacy system; state is not unified and data does not flow back. The cost is two competing sources of truth and a permanent argument over which version counts—the stronger the AI, the deeper the split.

Demo-driven development. A single successful demo is treated as acceptance; the occasional is mistaken for the capable. The cost is the absence of any regression baseline after launch, so every model or data change can silently degrade the system with nobody noticing.

Silent degradation and fabricated metrics. The interface is always green; failures are swallowed. The cost is a polluted decision chain, and trust can be overdrawn only once—once the data is exposed as invented, every real number afterward is doubted as well.

Prompt as architecture. Business rules are scattered through long prompts and versioned by copy-paste. The cost is no testability and no auditability; switching models breaks everything, and there is no type system in a prompt, no compiler to catch your mistakes.

Chapter 2 Open Complex Giant Systems: The Epistemology of AI-Native Development

Author: Quantum Swordsman Volume I · The Case for AI-Native

1. The Problem: Why the Waterfall Must Fail on Coupled Systems

At an early review of a vehicle program, each department reports in turn: styling says proportions are unsettled, packaging says hard points are not frozen, manufacturing says stamping feasibility is pending, and the brand team wants another round on DNA. The project manager demands that requirements be "written clearly before development begins." The minutes go through three revisions, the requirements document grows thicker, and consensus grows thinner. This is not incompetence. It is a misdiagnosis of the problem's nature.

Automotive styling is shaped simultaneously by market context, brand genes, engineering hard points, manufacturing processes, cultural aesthetics, and user emotion—and these forces keep evolving. In Qian Xuesen's terminology this is an open complex giant system. Open: the system exchanges information with its environment continuously, and aesthetic drift and technology turnover will not pause while the project freezes requirements. Giant: the number of subsystems is enormous—parameters, surfaces, components, data flows, decision points. Complex: the couplings between subsystems are nonlinear and multilayered; wheelbase shifts proportion, proportion shifts the aesthetics score, and the score constrains the wheelbase range in return. The reductionist recipe—divide, optimize each part, sum—fails here because what gets cut away is precisely the relationships between parts, and a vehicle is a product of those relationships.

2. Principles

Principle 1: The whole comes first; divide work only after the totality exists. A complex giant system cannot be summed into existence; it must be organized into existence. The engineering meaning is concrete: freeze layering, boundaries, and interface contracts first, then let modules evolve independently. The totality does not do the subsystems' work, but it determines the shape in which subsystems are assembled. Reverse the order and every module is locally optimal while the integration phase cancels them out.

Principle 2: Layers are distinct; govern each with its own laws. Styling cognition has at least three layers—imagery (qualitative), parameters (quantitative), surfaces (geometric)—with engineering delivery below. Between layers stands translation, not magnification: pouring the language of imagery directly into geometry yields noise, not design. Each layer needs its own model, its own criteria, its own verification.

Principle 3: Openness is embraced; environmental feedback is input, not noise. The boundary of an open system is a channel for exchange, not a wall for defense. Market feedback, aesthetic drift, and stack turnover should be modeled as continuous signal streams. Treating feedback as noise is equivalent to running a closed-loop experiment in an open environment: the system's world model falls steadily behind the world.

Principle 4: Evolution is dynamic; replace one-shot planning with feedback loops. There is no day of "requirements freeze" in a complex giant system—only current beliefs and the next correction. The unit of governance is not the phase but the loop: suggest, evaluate, feed back, converge, distill. Every turn deepens the system's understanding of the problem. Planning sets the loop's direction; it does not prophesy the loop's destination.

When this does not apply: during divergent exploration—when no objective function exists and feedback has no criterion—the loop yields to diversity sampling. Produce variance first; build the loop afterward.

3. Case Anatomy: How the Platform Catches Each Property

EVOLUTION AI is itself a complex giant system on the platform side: 19 route modules, 154 HTTP endpoints (152 routed plus 2 system), 17 ORM tables, and 12 frontend pages. Its design decisions can be mapped one by one onto giant-system properties—which is what "epistemology landing in engineering" means.

Catching magnitude. A vehicle is driven by the 22-dimensional CarParams and assembled into a 10-part preview mesh of 176 vertices and 316 faces, plus 33 NURBS surfaces. Exhausting this combinatorial space by hand is impossible; the platform's answer is parametric generation (a full vehicle in seconds) with batch builds and cache management. Scale is delegated to the machine; people govern the shape of the space.

Catching complexity. Strong coupling between parameters is the root of styling difficulty: changing the wheelbase alters proportion, stance, and several angle constraints at once. The platform does not enumerate coupling with rules; it learns the coupling structure from observations with a Gaussian-process surrogate (RBF kernel, ℓ=0.25), then lets EI/UCB acquisition functions decide where to sample next. Coupling is not eliminated; it is modeled.

Catching hierarchy. The four layers—imagery, parameters, surfaces, engineering—each have carriers: the brand knowledge base and intent parsing for imagery, the 22-dimension parameter panel for parameters, the dual geometry core for surfaces, and STEP/IGES export with continuity reports for engineering. Inter-layer translation uses explicit interfaces; direct cross-layer access is not allowed.

Catching openness. The brand knowledge base (5 brands, 19 models) is extensible, the parameter space is evolvable, and every review score flows back through the observe endpoint as a GP observation. New information from the environment is not an interruption; it is fuel.

Catching incomplete predictability. Real aesthetic preference cannot be known a priori, so every exploration is organized as a qualitative-to-quantitative leap (developed in Chapter 3), while roughly 1,027 tests (200 algorithm, 411 backend, 416 frontend—as of 2026-10-25, per docs/VALIDATION_REPORT_20261025.md) lock down already-verified behavior—what can be verified is not left to luck, and what cannot is not pretended certain.

Cross-domain mapping. The governance laws of giant systems do not depend on the styling context:

  • In this platform = review scores flow back through the observe endpoint as GP observations, and the system's belief updates with the environment; in LLM evaluation = baselines are locked, live distributions are monitored, and drift triggers alarms and re-baselining. Both are "openness embraced": as the world changes, the system's world model needs a formal update channel, not word of mouth.
  • In this platform = strong parameter coupling is learned by a surrogate rather than enumerated by rules, and what cannot be predicted (real aesthetic preference) is not pretended certain; in a general-purpose agent = dependencies among tools are modeled explicitly by the orchestration layer, and an unavailable tool is reported as unavailable, never disguised as success. Coupling and unpredictability are not eliminated; they are acknowledged and located.

4. Antipatterns and Their Cost

The reductionist waterfall. Freeze requirements, build separately, integrate, accept. In a coupled system, the severed relationships return as rework during integration; the thicker the requirements document, the farther it drifts from the real problem.

Feature-list accumulation. Platform building is understood as linear addition of features—see a need, add a module. The cost is growing structural entropy: more modules, fewer people able to answer what the system as a whole is doing.

Feedback treated as noise. Review comments and market shifts are blocked as "distractions." The cost is a steadily depreciating world model—not one wrong decision, but an entire expired map beneath every decision.

Layer trespassing. Letting imagery-layer language ("make it sportier") directly drive geometry-layer edits, or sending geometry-layer artifacts (curvature combs) straight into management reports. Every layer loses its own criteria, and meetings become three languages arguing in one room.

Chapter 3 From Qualitative to Quantitative: The Metasynthesis Method

Author: Quantum Swordsman Volume I · The Case for AI-Native

1. The Problem: How Does "Elegant and Restrained" Enter an Optimizer?

At a review meeting the design director says: "This version must be elegant and restrained." The engineer returns to a desk where an optimizer accepts only numbers. The sentence cannot be fed into any algorithm—it has no dimensions, no units, no bounds. Tradition offers two solutions, both undignified. The first is to let the engineer "translate" the sentence into a few parameter adjustments by feel, a translation that leaves no trace. The second is to ignore the sentence entirely and let the optimizer converge in a purely numeric space, producing a batch of proposals that score high but do not look like cars.

This is the core question the methodology of complex giant systems must answer: between qualitative judgment and quantitative computation lies not merely a format gap but two kinds of knowledge. Qian Xuesen's metasynthesis—from the qualitative to the quantitative—offers the path: organize expert judgment (qualitative), data and models (quantitative), and machine computation (tooling) into one system, and approach a scientific understanding of the complex problem iteratively—human-machine collaboration, with the human in command. This chapter discusses how that method becomes a running software system rather than a slogan.

2. Principles

Principle 1: Qualitative and quantitative supply each other. Imagery selects the structure of the parameter space—which dimensions exist, where the bounds lie, which dimensions are frozen. Quantitative scores correct the landing point of imagery—which region of the space "elegant and restrained" actually occupies is decided by observed data. Without either end, the loop degenerates: qualitative only is open-loop discussion; quantitative only is blind search without priors.

When this does not apply: during divergent exploration—before any objective function exists—"the quantitative correcting the qualitative" has nothing to act on. Sample for diversity first; close the loop once the objective takes shape.

Principle 2: Three bodies, three jobs, in relay. The expert system poses problems, sets boundaries, and makes value judgments; the machine system performs massive exploration, exact computation, and pattern recognition; the knowledge system stores, retrieves, and reactivates experience. These are not three voters in parallel but a relay in series: the qualitative shapes the search space, the quantitative finds solutions within the shape, and knowledge distills each solution into the next round's prior.

Principle 3: Beliefs are revisable; a model is the current state of understanding. A surrogate model is not truth but a formalization of "what we currently believe." Every observation updates it—homologous to the cycle of practice, knowledge, practice again. The engineering requirement: the model must expose its own uncertainty, not emit a bare score, because the uncertainty itself is the guide for the next sample.

Principle 4: Samples are precipitated knowledge. An optimization session should yield not only an optimum but the full observation record that led to it. That record is the fossil of each qualitative-to-quantitative leap: it can be replayed as training data so that the next search stands on the previous one's shoulders—individual cognition is thus promoted to organizational cognition.

3. Case Anatomy: The Platform's Working Loop

EVOLUTION AI's fundamental working loop is metasynthesis implemented in software. On the qualitative end, "elegant and restrained" passes through design-intent parsing and the brand knowledge base (5 brands, 19 models) and lands as the structure of a parameter space: which 14 of the 22 CarParams dimensions enter the search, what bounds each takes, and which 8 are frozen as regulatory and structural hard points. On the quantitative end, quality evaluation translates "good or not" into regression-safe numbers: G0/G1/G2 counts, reflection-line scores, and an A–D composite grade, with tolerances of 0.1 mm / 1.0° and thresholds of 5° for G1 and 2° for G2. The graduations of the ruler are defined by engineering consensus; the machine merely applies the same ruler every time.

Between the two ends sits a Gaussian-process surrogate (RBF kernel, ℓ=0.25), which organizes observed parameter-to-score pairs into the current belief, emitting a posterior mean and an uncertainty. EI/UCB acquisition functions make rational decisions on top of that belief: with fewer than two observations the engine draws random space-filling points; afterward it generates a pool of 4096 candidates in the unit cube, ranks them by acquisition value, and returns suggestions. Scores flow back through POST /api/v1/bayes/sessions/{id}/observations, the belief updates, and the loop continues with the EI minimum-improvement threshold 0.01 (ei_xi) as its stopping reference; the plateau—empirically reached within 20 to 40 rounds—is confirmed by session observation and human judgment. The code carries no converged field and no automatic convergence verdict yet; automatic dual-criterion convergence is a roadmap item.

The decisive part is the lower half of the loop: exported samples carry a feature_order identical to the training pipeline's, so they replay directly as a supervised dataset driving the PyTorch training tasks in backend/app/routes/training.py. Search (the optimizer proposes candidates) and learning (observations precipitate into training data) thereby close into one ring: everything the Nth round of optimization learned becomes the starting point of round N+1.

Cross-domain mapping. The metasynthesis loop has a strict homologue in language-model systems:

  • In this platform = the scoring rubric is defined by engineering consensus (G1 threshold 5°, G2 threshold 2°, tolerances 0.1 mm / 1.0°), and the machine applies the same ruler every time; in LLM evaluation = humans lock the scoring baseline, the same input reproduces the same score, and distribution drift raises an alarm. Both are "the qualitative supplies the ruler; the quantitative does the measuring."
  • In this platform = the Gaussian process exposes posterior uncertainty, and that uncertainty directs the next sample; in a RAG system = when retrieval confidence is insufficient, the system declares "I don't know" rather than forcing an answer. Uncertainty is not hidden; it is consumed as a decision input.

4. Antipatterns and Their Cost

Pure numeric optimization without priors. Skip the qualitative translation and search the full parameter space for score. The cost is convergence to solutions that "score high but do not look like cars"—the objective encodes no brand or proportion priors, and the optimizer is merely honestly maximizing the wrong objective.

Machine verdict replacing human judgment. The ranking of composite scores is taken directly as the review conclusion. The cost is outsourced value judgment to an entity that cannot be held accountable; once the objective deviates from true intent, the entire team marches systematically in the direction of the deviation without knowing it.

Knowledge drifting from code. Experience stays in documents, documents stay in directories, while parameters and thresholds quietly change in code—the classic documentation drift. The cost is a steadily depreciating prior: the next search runs against the previous version of the world, and nobody announced that the world had changed.

One-shot cleverness. Every optimization session starts from zero; observation records are discarded after use. The cost is that organizational cognition never accumulates: when an expert retires, the experience retires too; when a project closes, its lessons close with it. A loop without sedimentation is merely warming up, no matter how many turns it completes.

Chapter 4 Five-Layer Architecture and Thin Orchestration

Author: Quantum Swordsman Volume II · Architecture: Giving Complexity Its Proper Place

1. The Problem: Which Layer Should Complexity Live In?

A familiar accident scene: the frontend drags the "vehicle length" slider, the request lands in some route handler on the backend, and inside that handler sit three hundred lines of geometry computation that write to the database midway and return a bare dict on failure. Six months later nobody dares touch the function—the geometry expert dare not, because authentication is wired in; the backend engineer dare not, because transactions are wired in; the frontend dare not, because the response shape is historical. The complexity has not disappeared. It has simply moved into the wrong floor.

The old sermon about layered architecture gains a new reason in AI-native systems: AI components keep evolving, while web contracts must stay stable. If geometry algorithms grow inside HTTP handlers, every algorithm upgrade is an interface gamble. If authorization logic seeps into the algorithm package, every permission change re-runs the numeric test suite. The proposition of this chapter: the web layer only orchestrates, and the algorithm kernel can be installed, tested, and replaced independently. Complexity goes to its proper floor not by convention but by hard constraints on dependency direction.

2. Principles

Principle 1: Dependencies point one way; lower layers never look up. The value of layering is not a prettier diagram but monotonic dependency: an upper layer may call a lower one, and a lower layer must not import a single symbol from above. Once this holds, every layer can be understood and replaced alone. Once it is broken—even by one "convenient" backward import—layering degenerates into folder naming.

Principle 2: The web layer is a thin shell; orchestration does not compute. An HTTP handler has a fixed list of duties: validate parameters, authorize, persist, orchestrate, map errors. It contains no geometry formulas, no optimization mathematics, no hand-rolled business rules. The test is plain: if a handler exceeds the three-beat rhythm of receive—delegate—return, it is secretly growing a second heart.

When this does not apply: systems that need a full business-workflow engine—branching, long transactions, human approval interleaved—where the thin shell must yield to an explicit state machine and the receive–delegate–return rhythm no longer suffices.

Principle 3: The algorithm kernel is independently installable. Algorithmic capability is the system's heavy asset. It deserves its own package structure, its own entry points, its own dependency manifest, and its own test suite. Whether pip install plus the full numeric test suite runs on a machine with no database and no web framework is the touchstone of algorithmic independence.

The precise boundary: L4's core kernel is pure NumPy, installable and testable standalone via pip. Deep learning and large models are not part of the kernel; they are optional, losable plug-ins—an enhancement when present, a degradation when absent, never a crash. The division criterion follows: orchestration (routing, auth, persistence, tasks, secrets) lives in L3; inference, prompt strategy, scoring, and training live in L4 or the optional-capability layer. The evidence is behavioral: without PyTorch, training endpoints return 503—honest degradation, not a process crash.

When this does not apply: hyperscale GPU training—thousand-card scheduling and throughput optimization exceed the reach of a pure-NumPy kernel; that concern belongs to the optional plug-in layer, where the kernel principle does not apply.

Principle 4: Capability boundaries are drawn between layers. Detection and degradation of optional capabilities (PyTorch, CLIP, Redis, LLMs) must happen on inter-layer interfaces: the lower layer truthfully reports what it lacks, and the upper layer chooses a degradation path and labels it. Failures are not hidden inside layers; success is not fabricated between them.

3. Case Anatomy: Five Layers, Each in Its Place

EVOLUTION AI divides into five layers. L1, the frontend: Vue 3.4 + Vite 5 + Three.js 0.166, twelve hash-routed pages. L2, access: Vite Dev Proxy in development, Nginx in production, a unified /api/v1 prefix, and a cpolar HTTPS tunnel for public exposure. Two views—do not read them as one. The "four layers plus a pipeline" of Figure 4-2 (imagery, parameters, surfaces, engineering) is a responsibility view, divided by deliverable. The L1–L5 layers here are a deployment view, divided by runtime boundary. They do not conflict: "backend" in the responsibility view expands into L2 (access) and L3 (backend services) here, while "data" expands into L5; "frontend" and "algorithm" are the same in both. Whenever you cite a layer count, state which view you mean.

Take the Bayesian module: the seven endpoints in backend/app/routes/bayes.py do exactly four things—validate the parameter space (non-empty, unique names, lower bound below upper), delegate to the session registry in bayes_optimizer.py, map the optimizer's explicit exceptions to HTTP 400/404/409, and persist observations. Cross-domain mapping. Layering and inter-layer honesty do not depend on a geometry kernel:

  • In this platform = capability probing happens between layers: the lower layer reports what is missing, the upper layer degrades and labels it (training endpoints return 503 without PyTorch); in a general-purpose agent = when a tool is unavailable, the orchestration layer reports "unavailable" truthfully instead of faking success.   • In this platform = three suites (200 algorithm / 411 backend / 416 frontend) run per layer, so one layer can be replaced without disturbing the others; in LLM evaluation = unit evals, chain evals, and end-to-end baselines are locked per layer, and drift in one layer alarms alone. The fat controller. Business logic inside route handlers; geometry, transactions, and authorization tangled together. The cost: changing any one of the three requires understanding all of them, regression failures cannot be localized, and the function becomes a legacy nobody dares touch.

It saves one parameter passing and places the weight of the whole building on a single point of the foundation—afterward, every independent L4 test must first erect L3 scaffolding.

Fabricated success between layers. A lower dependency goes missing and the upper layer pads the response with fake data. The cost, as Chapter 1 established: trust can be overdrawn only once. The inter-layer interface is the last place in the system where the truth can still be told; if it falls, honesty elsewhere is meaningless.

Chapter 5 One Vector, Four Expressions: The Dual Geometry Core

Author: Quantum Swordsman Volume II · Architecture: Giving Complexity Its Proper Place

1. The Problem: Why Is "What You See Is What You Deliver" So Hard?

There is a tacit fault line in the daily work of styling departments: what is seen in the review meeting and what the engineering department receives are not the same thing. Previews run on triangle meshes—fast, fluid, rotatable in real time. Delivery requires NURBS surfaces—precise, smoothable, acceptable to downstream CAD. The traditional bridge between them is called reverse fitting: deriving surfaces from meshes. Fitting always has residuals, residuals always demand manual repair, and manual repair is never reproducible. Every line a designer edits on the preview side must be "translated" again on the engineering side, and translation is never symmetric.

The root of the fault line is not a shortage of tools but expressions that do not share a source. Mesh and surface are driven by two separate bodies of data, evolve separately, and are aligned by hand. The proposition of this chapter: one vector, three families of derivatives—geometric expressions, quality metrics, and exchange formats, all forward-generated from the same parameter vector, eliminating secondary fitting. When all three families are pure functions of the same vector, "what you see is what you deliver" turns from slogan into theorem: the shape approved in the preview and the shape delivered in the STEP file are generated from the same parameters, with zero divergence.

2. Principles

Principle 1: One parameter source; everything else is derived. The system permits exactly one human-editable object—the parameter vector. Meshes, surfaces, scores, and export files are all derived from it, and derivation may carry no hidden state of its own. Every editing entry that "pulls a vertex directly on the mesh" or "patches a region by hand on the surface side" manufactures a second source of truth.

When this does not apply: non-parametric freeform modeling—sculptural modeling has no parameter vector to serve as the single source, and this principle yields to explicit version snapshots and diffing.

Principle 2: Expressions live downstream; take the family you need. The derivatives of one parameter vector come in three families. Geometric expressions: the preview mesh (10 parts, 176 vertices, 316 faces) and the NURBS surfaces (33 for a full vehicle). Quality metrics: G0/G1/G2 counts, reflection-line scores, and the A–D composite grade. Exchange formats: eight geometric formats (glb / gltf / stl / obj / iges / step / igs / stp), led by the dual links STL/GLB on the mesh side and STEP/IGES on the NURBS side, with OBJ/GLTF/IGS/STP kept for compatibility. Each family has its use, but none deserves the title "master expression"—they are pure-function projections of the same 22-dimensional vector, and which family you read depends on the question at hand.

Principle 3: Refuse secondary fitting; take the forward road to the end. Generate surfaces from parameters forward; do not fit surfaces from meshes backward. Forward generation has no fitting residual, and every designer edit propagates exactly into control-point changes. Backward fitting forever carries an irreducible approximation error that accumulates with every edit. Secondary fitting saves one-off development cost; what it pays is uncertainty in every iteration.

Principle 4: Tolerances come first; criteria are born with the construction. Quality standards are not an inspection procedure after construction but a constituent of the construction itself: continuity thresholds, sampling density, and grading rubrics are fixed at generation time. Problems found by after-the-fact inspection can only be reworked; criteria endogenous to construction can prevent them.

3. Case Anatomy: How Two Pipelines Share One Vector

In EVOLUTION AI, a vehicle is described by the 22-dimensional CarParams (algorithm_model/car_modeling/car_params.py), and this single object is the common input to two geometric pipelines.

The mesh path serves interaction. The parametric body is built from longitudinal and circumferential section rings (default discretization 48×24) and assembled with wheels, lamps, grille, mirrors, and glass into a 10-part vehicle mesh of 176 vertices and 316 faces, feeding the Three.js viewport for real-time preview and STL/GLB tessellated export. The assembler also reports per-part vertex and face counts, bounding boxes, and stable part colors so the frontend can render, identify, and count.

The NURBS path serves engineering. A separate builder constructs the body as true NURBS surfaces from the control mesh—33 surfaces for a full vehicle—with dedicated modules building front and rear end caps that meet the body with G1 tangent transitions. Companion freeform operators cover sweeping and filleting; STEP is emitted by a pure-Python writer (algorithm_model/freeform/step_writer.py) following ISO 10303-21, with no dependency on an Open CASCADE (OpenCascade)-scale kernel. The crucial point: surfaces are generated directly from parameters, not reverse-fitted from triangle meshes—there is no fitting residual.

Quality and export are downstream of the same source. The continuity checker (continuity_checker.py) samples the four parametric boundaries of each named surface (50 points per edge by default) and judges every seam against G0 0.1 mm and G1 1.0° thresholds, writing TXT/CSV reports; the surface-quality package produces G0/G1/G2 counts, reflection-line scores, and A–D grades on the mesh side. Two paths, two granularities, complementary criteria: exact NURBS continuity at the seams, plus curvature distribution across the tessellated body.

The book involves two sets of G0/G1/G2 thresholds, belonging to the two links respectively; they must not be mixed up:

Link Object checked G0 G1 G2 Unit Code source
Tessellated-mesh holistic evaluation Discrete mesh — 5° 2° degrees surface_quality/continuity.py, grader.py
NURBS seam-by-seam Adjacent surface patches 0.1 mm 1.0° (plus visual 5.0°) — mm/° car_modeling/continuity_checker.py (50 points per edge)

The chain thus closes: CarParams ──► mesh assembly ──► interactive preview / STL / GLB and CarParams ──► NURBS body + G1 end caps ──► G0/G1/G2 analysis ──► STEP/IGES. Every arrow is a pure function of the same parameter vector. The export engine actually supports eight geometric formats: STL/GLB as the primary mesh-pipeline outputs and STEP/IGES as the primary NURBS-pipeline outputs, with OBJ/GLTF/IGS/STP retained for compatibility (plus JSON parameter export). This is why the Bayesian optimizer of Chapter 3 can treat "styling plus quality" as a single evaluable function—suggest one parameter set and all three derivative families materialize at once; no representation needs manual reconstruction.

Cross-domain mapping. "Single source, pure-function derivation" has a strict homologue in evaluation:

  • In this platform = one 22-dimensional vector derives three families—geometry, quality, and formats—and any family can be recomputed from the same vector; in LLM evaluation = one locked baseline yields the same score for the same input, and any report can be replayed from the baseline. What "no secondary fitting" is called in evaluation is "results are replayable."
  • In this platform = deriving surfaces from meshes is forbidden because fitting residuals are irreproducible; in evaluation = deriving scores from reports is forbidden because retroactively patched scores carry no baseline authority. Reverse procedures save one-off cost and pay with credibility in every round.

4. Antipatterns and Their Cost

Bridging with secondary fitting. Preview on meshes, reverse-fit NURBS before delivery. The cost is fitting residuals plus manual repair: every design iteration repays the translation fee, and repairs are irreproducible—"how was this fixed last time" has no answer.

Two sources of truth. Edit one version in the parameter panel, then hand-pull another on the mesh side. Flexible in the short run, disastrous in the long: nobody can compute the difference between the two shapes, and between the version the review approved and the version engineering received yawns a gap of manual reconciliation.

Separate preview and delivery geometry. Simplified models for fluency in preview; refined models rebuilt for delivery. The cost is institutionalized "what you see is not what you get": what the review approved was never the thing to be manufactured.

Tolerances inspected after the fact. Build geometry casually, check quality before delivery. Problems can then only be reworked, not prevented; and rework near delivery has no time budget, so it ends in quality compromise.

Chapter 6. Contract-First Interface Design

Author: Quantum Swordsman Volume II · Architecture: Giving Complexity Its Proper Place

1. The Problem: "It Runs" Is Not "It Ships"

A demo needs one path that works. A deliverable system must answer a different set of questions: who may call this, what goes in, what comes out, what state the system is left in after failure, and whether the tenth module added next quarter will silently break the conventions of the first nine. Once an interface surface grows past a handful of endpoints, the dominant risk shifts from "did we build the feature" to "did we keep the agreement."

EVOLUTION AI carries a substantial surface: 19 route modules with 152 router endpoints, plus 2 system endpoints declared directly in main.py, for a total of 154 HTTP endpoints. Seventeen ORM tables hold persistent state. If every module invents its own field names, interprets status in its own way, and picks its own error codes, then 154 endpoints become 154 local dialects, and every frontend integration becomes an exercise in re-learning. Contract-first design is the discipline that collapses local dialect into a globally testable agreement.

The proposition of this chapter is: endpoints, data tables, and state machines precede implementation, and 100 percent field alignment is a hard constraint. "Precede" is not a slogan about writing documents before code. It means the shape of the interface must be reviewable, testable, and regression-safe before any business logic is written.

2. Principles

Principle 1. The three-layer contract comes before implementation

Every endpoint has three layers of contract. The call contract defines method, path, and authentication level. The data contract defines request and response fields, types, and valid ranges. The state contract defines legal states and legal transitions. Once these three are fixed, implementation is filling in the blanks; while they remain fluid, more implementation experience only produces more thorough rework. The platform expresses the data contract with FastAPI 0.104 and Pydantic v2, keeps all request and response models in backend/app/schemas.py, and exposes an OpenAPI description at /docs. Authentication levels are declared through dependency injection rather than scattered if statements inside handlers.

Principle 2. Fields align completely; conversion happens only at the boundary

The backend uses snake_case and the frontend uses camelCase. These are naming habits of two languages, not two data models. The platform converts once, at the boundary: the snakeToCamel helper in the designer store normalizes underscored fields from the backend, and pages and components see a single naming convention afterward. The training-sample contract follows the same rule. The feature_order exported by a Bayesian session must match the training pipeline position by position; otherwise "the samples are trainable" is a wish rather than a property.

When this does not apply: during the evolution of a public API—when external consumers cannot upgrade in lockstep with the repository—single-point hard alignment yields to versioned contracts (v1 and v2 side by side); field freezing binds only within one version.

Principle 3. State machines are explicit and error codes carry meaning

State must not be inferred from a combination of booleans. A workflow may be pending, running, completed, or failed. A step may be pending, completed, or rejected. Workflow status is derived by aggregating step status, and there is no manual path for setting it. Failure must not be wrapped as 200 + {success:false}. A 400 means business validation failed, 401 means credentials are missing or invalid, 404 means the resource does not exist, 405 means the method is not allowed, 409 means a state conflict, 422 means schema validation failed, and 503 means an optional capability is not ready. When each failure has exactly one exit, the caller can write deterministic recovery logic.

Principle 4. Contracts must be executable and regression-tested

A contract written only in prose drifts. A contract expressed in types and tests raises an alarm. Of the platform's roughly 1,027 automated tests (200 algorithm, 411 backend, 416 frontend—as of 2026-10-25, per docs/VALIDATION_REPORT_20261025.md), the backend suite covers route contracts, parameter validation, and failure branches, while the frontend suite covers API modules and store-level field mapping. The contract is not a consensus reached in a review meeting; it is a gate that every commit must pass again.

3. Case Study: How 154 Endpoints Speak One Language

Start with the authentication contract. The 152 router endpoints fall into four tiers: 114 public, 9 optional-auth, 12 required-auth, and 17 admin. This is not an arbitrary tally; it is the structural result of "reads stay mostly open, writes converge to authentication, governance converges to admin." Guest mode lets unauthenticated requests through as guests, yet admin endpoints still demand admin-level dependencies. The same dependency-injection stack serves both open reading and high-privilege governance, so no route re-explains permissions on its own.

Next, the chain contract. The main chain from login to token-based training to workflow review spans four modules — auth, training, workflow, and project — covering 41 endpoints. The chain actually calls only 6 contracts, 14.6 percent. The remaining 35 endpoints are management and extension capabilities that the chain never triggers. The size of the interface surface need not scale with the complexity of the chain: with clear contract boundaries, a small chain can safely consume a large surface.

The Bayesian module shows a different contract density. It has only 7 endpoints, yet it writes the session lifecycle in full. Creation validates that the space is non-empty, names are unique, and every lower bound is below its upper bound, else 400. Querying a missing session returns 404. Asking for the best observation before any exist returns 409, not a fabricated empty optimum. Reporting an observation with unknown, missing, or out-of-bounds parameters is always 400. Before two observations, suggestions come from random space-filling; afterward, from the Gaussian process. State and algorithm phase are expressed by one contract, so the caller never has to guess "can I ask for a suggestion yet."

Brand DNA turns the contract into an exchangeable schema. BrandDNASchema pins version 1.0, requires brand and on_brand_constraints at the top level, allows only PASS, FAIL, NO_DATA, and N/A as verdicts, and converges rule types to zero, range, enum, and sealed. Most important is the semantics of NO_DATA: when something cannot be measured now, it is labeled as such and never presumed to pass. Here the contract constrains not just fields but honesty itself.

Cross-domain mapping. The honesty semantics of contracts hold verbatim in language-model systems:

  • In this platform = querying the best before any observation returns 409, and Brand DNA labels what cannot be measured as NO_DATA rather than presuming a pass; in a RAG system = when retrieval finds no basis, the answer is "I don't know," not a fabrication. Empty states must have named exits—a contract constrains honesty, not just fields.
  • In this platform = an unready optional capability returns 503 with a reason; in a general-purpose agent = an unavailable tool is reported as unavailable, never answered with a forged success. Semantic error codes and "never fake success" are one contractual discipline.

4. Anti-Patterns and Their Costs

Anti-pattern 1: Improvised interface growth. Write the page first, then add whatever endpoint it happens to need, naming fields for the convenience of the moment. The cost is that endpoints multiply faster than understanding; every new module integration means reading source to guess semantics, and 154 endpoints become 154 oral traditions.

Anti-pattern 2: Dual-track fields. snake_case on the backend, a re-invented camelCase on the frontend, mapping tables maintained on both sides. Nothing breaks at first; over time a field is inevitably changed on one side only. Such bugs throw no exception — they just make a chart quietly lose a series.

Anti-pattern 3: State hidden in boolean combinations. Three booleans — is_running, is_done, has_error — give eight combinations, of which only four are meaningful. The cost is a state machine that tests cannot exhaust, with rejections and cancellations falling into undefined intermediate states.

Anti-pattern 4: Wrapping failure in 200. Every response returns 200 and distinguishes outcomes with success:false. The cost is that gateways, monitoring, and client retry policies all stop working: 503 no longer means capability unready, 409 no longer means conflict, and diagnosis requires reading business code.

5. Operational Checklist

  1. Before adding an endpoint, write down in the corresponding backend/app/routes/ module its method, path, auth level, request fields, response fields, and every failure code.
  2. For each resource, list legal states and legal transitions (following the step-aggregation pattern in workflow.py); never express state implicitly through boolean combinations.
  3. Keep a single naming-conversion point: snake_case to camelCase exactly once at the API boundary (see snakeToCamel in the designer store).
  4. Establish explicit constants for cross-module data orderings such as feature_order, and validate length and order at the consumer.
  5. Constrain boundary values with Pydantic models in schemas.py; do not substitute ad-hoc checks inside function bodies.
  6. Assign each error code exactly one triggering condition; the same failure must never return multiple status codes.
  7. Write contract tests covering at least: success path, boundary values, unknown fields, missing fields, out-of-range values, and state conflicts (see backend/tests/test_bayes.py and src/tests/api.spec.js).
  8. When a contract changes, update OpenAPI, the frontend src/api.js, and tests together — never ship them in separate batches.

6. Chapter Self-Check

  1. If all implementation code were deleted, leaving only the endpoint tables, schemas, and state machines, could a newcomer say precisely under what conditions each endpoint returns 400, 404, 409, or 503?
  2. Does your frontend convert fields exactly once at the boundary, or is every page guessing backend field names on its own?
  3. When an optional capability is missing, does your API return 503 with a reason — or a plausible-looking fake success?

Chapter 7. The Unified Workbench: Information Architecture of 12 Pages

Author: Quantum Swordsman Volume II · Architecture: Giving Complexity Its Proper Place

1. The Problem: More Pages, Less Truth?

Frontend projects have a common way of dying. The first page is polished. By the fifth page, everyone writes their own way. By the twelfth, the same project data exists in three versions across three pages, and the same car looks different in two views. Page count itself is not the crime. The disease is that every page hoards its own copy of state, its own slice of logic, and its own private understanding of the backend. In the late stages, the team no longer debates "what does the user see" but "which page is telling the truth."

EVOLUTION AI has 12 routed pages: Dashboard, Designer, DesignCopilot, Projects, ProjectDetail, DeepLearning, Quality, Deliver, Demo, Help, Community, and Contact. Together they share 154 backend endpoints — about 12.8 per page. If each page organized its own data and logic, twelve pages would amount to twelve small backends. The proposition of this chapter is: pages do not restate geometry logic, and state has exactly one source of truth. A unified workbench is not a unified visual style; it is a unified information architecture. Pages handle presentation and interaction only; data flows from a single store layer, a single API layer, and a single backend contract.

2. Principles

Principle 1. Four-stage orchestration, with pages as the thinnest stage

The full journey of one click is: page → store → API → 19 route modules / 154 endpoints → 17 tables. Page components contain no geometry, no business rules, and no field guessing. They read state from Pinia stores, call the per-domain interfaces exported from src/api.js (projectAPI through bayesAPI), and hand results to rendering. Algorithms converge behind the backend routes, in the algorithm_model layer. When a page needs a new capability, the first question should be "which API domain owns this," not "let me compute it inside the component."

Principle 2. State has a single source of truth

Four stores serve the whole site: auth, designer, project, and ui. Every piece of data has one authoritative home, and pages hold references, not copies. The API layer is equally singular: api.js maintains one axios instance (baseURL /api/v1), injects the JWT uniformly, and handles 401 uniformly — clearing the token and broadcasting the evoai:auth-required event to drive a global login recovery, instead of twelve pages each improvising their own redirect.

Principle 3. Degradation must be visible in the interface

The unity of a workbench includes a unified way of failing. When the backend is unreachable, the project list falls back to mock data labeled as such. When PyTorch, Ollama, CLIP, or Redis is missing, the corresponding capability area shows a 503 or a "geometric features only" degradation label — never a counterfeit success. Guest mode keeps the whole site readable, with a login prompt appearing when a write is attempted. Capabilities vary in strength; the interface never blurs that fact.

Principle 4. Copy and language are architecture too

The platform carries full Chinese-English bilingual copy through vue-i18n. Internationalization is not a translation sprint before release; it is part of the information architecture: a term in both languages points to the same contract field, and knowledge-base documents switch with the platform language. A system whose languages disagree is two systems sharing one domain name.

3. Case Study: How 12 Pages Share One Truth

Take Designer, the platform's core workbench. It offers car-type, style, and brand parameter selection; carAPI.generate builds the vehicle with live 3D rendering; and cloud capabilities — dataset statistics, synthetic sample batches, quality evaluation, generative design, parameter optimization — all go through aiAPI. Deliver shows a different kind of unity. The page's information structure mirrors the backend's session contract directly, rather than inventing a private frontend model.

DeepLearning and Quality share the pattern of "polling one truth." After a training task is created, the page polls GET /ai/tasks/{id} once per second until a terminal state; quality reports are read from qualityAPI.list, with historical string reports normalized into structured display. State lives in the backend; pages are observers on a timeline. This is also why the frontend's 416 of the ~1,027 tests are possible at all: they target stores, API modules, and guards, not pixels.

  • Here = twelve pages share one Pinia store and one set of API modules; no page keeps a private copy. In a general agent console = session state, tool calls, and artifact preview all derive from one runtime state, so switching views never creates a second truth.
  • Here = writes give optimistic feedback, roll back on failure, and report the error explicitly. In an LLM evaluation panel = a rejected score submission must revert to the unsubmitted state rather than leave a fake success on screen.

4. Anti-Patterns and Their Costs

Anti-pattern 1: Pages hoarding state copies. Every page copies backend data into local variables and processes it privately. The cost is multiple versions of the same data and an endless dispute over "which page is authoritative."

Anti-pattern 2: Business logic embedded in components. Geometry conversions, permission checks, and field mappings written inside Vue components. The cost is logic that backend contracts cannot constrain and layered tests cannot cover; change one place, break three.

Anti-pattern 3: A request style per page. Some pages use axios, some use fetch, some concatenate URLs by hand. The cost is twelve dialects of authentication, error handling, and degradation — and a 401 that silently vanishes on one page.

Anti-pattern 4: Silent degradation. The backend is down, and the page shows an empty table or spins forever. The cost is that users misread "system failure" as "no data," and decisions get built on blank space.

5. Operational Checklist

  1. When a new page is proposed, first declare which stores and which API domains it consumes, in a header comment or document.
  2. Forbid geometry and business rules inside page components; when found, push them down to a store or the backend.
  3. All requests go through the single axios instance in src/api.js; pages may not build their own HTTP clients.
  4. Field-name conversion happens exactly once at the store boundary; raw backend naming must not appear inside pages.
  5. Define a degraded state for every page — what it shows and what it labels when the backend is unreachable — and implement it.
  6. Route all page copy through i18n keys; new terms enter both languages together.
  7. Page tests target stores and API modules (following the existing 416 cases in src/tests/), not pixels.
  8. Pages that write must use the shared evoai:auth-required login flow, not hand-rolled dialog logic.

6. Chapter Self-Check

  1. Pick any piece of business data. Can you name, within five seconds, which store holds its authoritative version and which endpoint it came from?
  2. If you deleted one page entirely, would backend contracts and all other pages be completely unaffected? If not, some logic is in the wrong layer.
  3. If the entire backend went down right now, what would your twelve pages each show — twelve accidents, or one designed degradation?

Chapter 8. Surrogate Models and Bayesian Optimization

Author: Quantum Swordsman Volume III · Intelligence: Putting Models into the System

1. The Problem: When Every Evaluation Is Expensive

Grid search and random search rest on a hidden assumption: evaluating the objective once costs almost nothing. Automotive styling development violates that assumption. Scoring one set of styling parameters means building the vehicle and running the quality evaluator — hundreds of milliseconds per shot in software; in real engineering, one evaluation may be a CFD run, a clay model, or an expert review, costing hours or weeks. In a world of expensive evaluations, deciding which parameter set deserves the next evaluation is itself the most valuable decision.

Brute force hits two walls here: the dimension wall and the cost wall. EVOLUTION AI's styling search space has 14 continuous dimensions (carved out of the 22-dimensional CarParams, with the 8 dimensions carrying size, regulation, and structural hard points frozen). Even ten grid points per dimension would mean 10^14 evaluations. The proposition of this chapter is: when evaluation is expensive, trade a GP surrogate plus an acquisition function for data efficiency, and keep convergence judgment defensible rather than automatic. The essence of Bayesian optimization is not cleverer sampling; it is pricing uncertainty — every unit of evaluation budget must buy back the most information possible.

2. Principles

Principle 1. Build the surrogate first, then talk about search

The first weapon of Bayesian optimization is not the acquisition function but the surrogate: a cheap probabilistic model that approximates the expensive true objective. The platform uses a Gaussian process with an RBF kernel, isotropic length scale ℓ=0.25 in normalized space, diagonal jitter of 1e-6, and Cholesky-based solves — the whole engine runs on NumPy and SciPy alone. The value of a GP is not raw fitting accuracy; it is that it returns a posterior mean and an uncertainty together. It knows where it does not know, and that is the precondition for pricing exploration.

When this does not apply: when observations are cheap and massively parallel, a surrogate is surplus — brute-force grid search is cheaper. A GP surrogate only pays off in a world where every evaluation is expensive.

Principle 2. The acquisition function prices exploration

With mean and uncertainty in hand, the acquisition function answers "where to sample next." Expected Improvement (EI, ξ=0.01) favors regions with both room to improve and high uncertainty; Upper Confidence Bound (UCB, κ=2.0) lifts the posterior mean by κσ across the board and is more willing to visit unsampled areas. Both are maximized over a candidate pool of 4096 random points — a derivative-free, naturally parallel, deliberately simple optimizer. Minimization objectives are negated internally so that one shared maximization path serves both goals.

Principle 3. Cold start must be honest

With fewer than two observations, the GP carries no information, and any "model suggestion" would be posturing. The engine therefore performs random space-filling in this phase and says so explicitly; only after two observations does it switch to GP-driven acquisition. An honest cold start is the "signal first" principle restated inside the algorithm.

Principle 4. Convergence criteria must be defensible

"Not improving anymore" is not the same as "converged." The platform tracks two reference signals. First, the best score is monotonically non-decreasing across rounds and enters a plateau where the slope approaches zero — empirically within 20–40 rounds. Second, the EI acquisition value falls below the 0.01 minimum-improvement threshold (ei_xi), meaning the expected gain from further exploration is negligible. Watching only the score mistakes a local plateau for the finish line; watching only EI ignores evaluation noise. In the current code the loop uses the EI minimum improvement 0.01 as its stopping reference, and the plateau or convergence call is made by session observation and human judgment — there is no converged field and no automatic convergence verdict. Automatic dual-criterion convergence [Partially Implemented — the EI stopping reference is in place, the automatic verdict is not] is a roadmap item, not a shipped behavior.

3. Case Study: The Full Lifecycle of One Session

The platform's Bayesian module has only 7 endpoints, yet it makes the loop of "suggest → evaluate → report → converge → export" a reproducible first-class citizen.

Step one, create the session. POST /bayes/sessions carries the parameter space (14 dimensions by default, with engineering bounds such as length 4000–6200 and wheelbase 2400–3700), the goal direction, the acquisition function, and a random seed. An empty space, duplicate names, or max <= min all return 400 — the contract stops illegal experiments at the door.

Step two, suggest. suggest returns parameter sets guaranteed inside the bounds. With fewer than two observations this is honest random filling; afterward it is GP-plus-acquisition targeted suggestion.

Step three, evaluate for real. The suggested parameters are used to build the vehicle and scored through /ai/evaluate-quality as an engineering proxy, or handed to human review. This is the only expensive step in the loop, and it is the one all data efficiency exists to serve.

Step four, report back. observe requires parameters covering all 14 dimensions of the space; unknown, missing, or out-of-bounds values return 400. On receipt, the GP retrains immediately and beliefs update.

Step five, converge and export. best returns the current optimum (409 when no observation exists — never a fabricated empty best); samples exports every observation, with feature_order aligned position-by-position to the training pipeline's feature columns. An optimization campaign thus settles into a supervised training dataset.

Engine correctness is backed by experiment: on the controlled one-dimensional objective -(x-0.7)², both EI and UCB run 15 suggest–observe rounds, and both push the best-so-far past the -0.01 band, effectively reaching the known optimum. The 12 tests in backend/tests/test_bayes.py cover the session lifecycle, boundary validation, directional correctness, and export format. One caution: sessions live in process memory and are wiped on backend restart — a deliberate simplicity. Anything worth keeping must go through samples export; memory belongs to data, not to the process.

Cross-Domain Mapping.

  • Here = each geometry evaluation is expensive, so a GP surrogate plus an EI acquisition function spends the budget where it buys the most information. In LLM evaluation = annotation and human grading are expensive too, so active sampling picks the most informative items instead of scoring everything.
  • Here = feasibility filtering masks the candidate pool once and, when candidates run short, falls back visibly and records the statistics. In RAG retrieval budgeting = hard constraints prune candidates first, then value ranks them, and every discard is explainable.

Capability status. The suggest / observe / best loop and feasibility filtering are [Implemented · Test-Locked] (backend suite collects 411 cases, including 1 skip and 2 xfail). The 0.01 EI minimum improvement is a stopping reference, not a convergence criterion; automatic two-criterion convergence is a roadmap item [Partially Implemented].

4. Anti-Patterns and Their Costs

Anti-pattern 1: Random search masquerading as Bayesian optimization. No surrogate, no acquisition, just uniform sampling and luck. The cost is evaluation budget sprayed evenly across a 14-dimensional space where most regions are useless, multiplying the rounds to convergence.

Anti-pattern 2: Treating the surrogate as truth. Using the GP posterior mean directly as the quality score, skipping real evaluation. The cost is a model that lies confidently where it has no data, and an optimization that converges to a phantom optimum existing only in the surrogate.

Anti-pattern 3: Single-signal convergence. Declaring victory when the score curve flattens. The cost is mistaking a local plateau for the global end — or, conversely, abandoning a still-profitable region too early in the presence of noise.

Anti-pattern 4: Observation formats divorced from training. Field order, units, and conventions in the optimization log disagree with the training pipeline. The cost is that accumulated observations cannot be replayed as a training set; the bridge between search and learning is quietly dismantled.

5. Operational Checklist

  1. When defining a search space, write each dimension's name, unit, and engineering bounds (following the default 14-dimensional space); frozen hard-point dimensions stay out of the space.
  2. Fix the random seed for every experiment so the suggestion sequence is reproducible.
  3. Reported observations must cover every dimension of the space; reject incomplete ones outright.
  4. Default to EI; switch to UCB for multi-modal objectives or wider coverage, and record κ.
  5. Treat the score plateau and the EI minimum-improvement threshold (0.01, ei_xi) as stopping references; the convergence call itself is made by session observation and human judgment (automatic dual-criterion convergence is a roadmap item).
  6. Export and archive through samples as soon as a session converges; never rely on in-process memory.
  7. Before feeding exported samples into training, verify feature_order matches the training pipeline position by position.
  8. Maintain convergence tests for the optimizer (following the 15-round one-dimensional experiment) and rerun them on every change.

6. Chapter Self-Check

  1. Can your optimizer say why the next evaluation point deserves its budget? If not, it is just random search.
  2. When you declare an optimization converged, how many independent pieces of evidence do you hold? With only one, it is not yet time to declare.
  3. Can the observations from your last optimization campaign be replayed today as training data, exactly as recorded? If not, where did the bridge between search and learning break?

Chapter 9. The Deep Learning / LLM Fusion Layer

Author: Quantum Swordsman Volume III · Intelligence: Putting Models into the System

1. The Problem: Many Models, One System

By the middle of an AI platform's life, the model list is usually longer than the feature list: a classification network, a vision-language model, seven or eight commercial LLMs, a local expert model. Each model runs on its own, yet together they often form an integration disaster — keys scattered through frontend code, upstream URLs supplied by clients at will, and an interface that still reports "AI generated successfully" when some dependency was never installed. The more models, the more the system resembles a collage of demos rather than a deliverable product.

EVOLUTION AI's fusion layer faces four heterogeneous capabilities: a PyTorch car-type network, CLIP semantic scoring, seven commercial LLM providers, and a local NURBS expert chat. The proposition of this chapter is: one unified proxy, a fixed registry, SSRF safety, and honest degradation whenever something is missing. Fusion does not mean wiring up every model; it means giving every capability the same boundary, the same honesty, and the same observability.

2. Principles

Principle 1. Heterogeneous capabilities, isomorphic boundaries

Whether the backing is a neural network on a GPU or an API a thousand miles away, the shape presented to the rest of the system must be identical: an explicit capability endpoint, unified authentication and key resolution, consistent error semantics, and pollable task state. The frontend never needs to know whether a capability is local or remote — availability is a runtime property reported by health checks and capability endpoints, not an assumption hard-coded into the interface.

Principle 2. The registry is fixed on the server side

The LLM proxy's provider registry is a server-side constant: seven providers — Baidu ERNIE, Alibaba Qwen, Tencent Hunyuan, ByteDance Doubao, DeepSeek, Moonshot Kimi, and SiliconFlow — each with a verified base URL, a default model, declared capabilities (chat / embeddings / images), and a documentation link. Because upstream URLs are not client-supplied parameters, no caller can redirect the server toward an arbitrary internal address. The SSRF attack surface is eliminated structurally, not filtered layer by layer.

Principle 3. Keys resolve explicitly; absence is an error

Credentials resolve first from environment variables, otherwise from the per-user encrypted store — user API keys are persisted with Fernet symmetric encryption. When a key is missing, the system returns a clear 4xx and never falls back silently to a fake model. Request and response bodies pass through verbatim, upstream status codes propagate as-is, and a capability the registry does not declare returns 501. Honesty is not a UX refinement; it is a security property. A forged success is far more dangerous than an explicit failure.

Principle 4. Claims must align with executable code

The platform deliberately does not ship a QLoRA-style fine-tuning job runner, and its documents name that as future work. The capability list contains only what is implemented; planned capabilities stay out of the functional definition. This discipline constrains the team itself: every sentence the system says must be cashable against the source tree.

3. Case Study: Four Capability Lines, One Discipline

The PyTorch car-type network. A compact MLP maps the 14-dimensional normalized styling vector to car-type classes (48 hidden units, six classes in the shipped configuration). Training runs in a background daemon thread, so the HTTP API returns a task object immediately; the client polls status, progress, and logs, and may cancel. Tasks persist in the training_tasks table. The training dataset is synthetic but deterministic: centroids come from real preset centers, per-parameter noise scales control sampling, explicit style and brand transforms perturb designated dimensions, and a seeded generator keeps runs and tests reproducible. When PyTorch is absent, the capability endpoint reports that fact and training requests fail cleanly — instead of returning fabricated metrics.

CLIP six-axis semantics. The semantics module wraps openai/clip-vit-base-patch32 (lazy-loaded, about 605 MB, offline once the weights are cached) and defines six style axes, each paired with a Chinese label and a concrete English visual prompt — a deliberate prompt-engineering choice, because CLIP responds far better to concrete visual descriptions than to abstract adjectives. An image is encoded once and compared against precomputed text embeddings, yielding a [0,1] score per axis. When the weights are missing, the system falls back to geometric features and labels the response "geometric features only," never hiding the downgrade.

A unified proxy for seven LLM providers. Chat completions, embeddings, and image generation are uniformly exposed under /llm/{provider}/... with optional authentication. A fixed registry, encrypted keys, transparent status propagation, and honest capability declarations — all four principles above are cashed into code here.

A local NURBS expert chat. Behind /ai/chat sits a local Ollama runtime with NURBS and Class-A domain context. A designer can ask "why does this edge fail G1" without sending design data to any third party; a health endpoint reports whether the local runtime is available. Data never leaving the intranet is the entire reason this path exists.

Cross-Domain Mapping.

  • Here = missing capabilities degrade honestly (HTTP 503 without torch, semantic scoring declared unavailable without CLIP) and the caller labels it. In RAG = when retrieval finds no support, the answer is "I don't know" rather than a fluent invention.
  • Here = the LLM is an optional add-on; with it switched off, parametric modeling and export still work. In a general agent = the orchestration skeleton outlives any single model vendor, and swapping models does not change the tool contract.

Capability status. The pure-NumPy geometry and Bayesian kernels are [Implemented · Test-Locked]; the deep-learning and LLM side is an optional add-on [Partially Implemented]—training returns 503 without torch, and semantic scoring declares itself unavailable without a downloaded CLIP model.

4. Anti-Patterns and Their Costs

Anti-pattern 1: Client-supplied upstream addresses. Letting callers pass a base URL or arbitrary proxy target. The cost is an open SSRF gate, with the server reduced to an intranet scanner.

Anti-pattern 2: Keys in the frontend or in logs. API keys written into frontend storage, URL parameters, or request logs. The cost is a credential-leak surface spanning every browser and every log line.

Anti-pattern 3: Silent fallback to a fake model. When a key is missing or an upstream times out, returning template text that impersonates model output. The cost is a decision chain contaminated by fabrication — and once discovered, every genuine output loses trust along with it.

Anti-pattern 4: Slide-deck capabilities first. Filling the feature list with planned capabilities that do not exist in code. The cost is acceptance without evidence, and credit repeatedly overdrawn between demo and delivery.

5. Operational Checklist

  1. When onboarding a model, register it first on the server side: base URL, default model, declared capabilities, documentation link (following llm_proxy.py).
  2. Take upstream addresses only from registry constants; never accept any URL from a client.
  3. Fix the key-resolution order: environment variables, then per-user encrypted storage (Fernet); if both are empty, return 4xx.
  4. Give every capability a health or capability endpoint; the frontend renders availability from these, never from hard-coded assumptions.
  5. On missing dependencies, fail explicitly or degrade with a label (PyTorch → training fails; CLIP → geometric fallback with annotation).
  6. Run every long task as a background thread backed by a persistent task table; return a task object immediately, with polling and cancellation.
  7. Seed the training-data generator so runs and tests are reproducible.
  8. Audit the capability list against the source tree item by item; anything unimplemented appears only under future work.

6. Chapter Self-Check

  1. If one LLM provider goes down tomorrow, at which layer and with which status code does your system tell the user? Will the user see "failure" — or "success but blank"?
  2. Can you draw your key-resolution order, from environment variable to encrypted store, on one diagram? If not, it was never designed — it merely happened.
  3. Is there anything in your feature list that the source tree cannot cash? If so, move it to future work — today.

Chapter 10. Semantic Interfaces: From Texture to Brand DNA

Author: Quantum Swordsman Volume III · Intelligence: Putting Models into the System

1. The Problem: How Aesthetic Judgment Enters an Engineering Process

The most expensive sentences at a styling review are the ones no machine can execute: "make it more aggressive," "this doesn't look like our brand," "the lamp signature needs to be sharper." These judgments are real, important, and impossible to refute — and equally impossible to regress. A design deemed "very on-brand" last week becomes "not quite on-brand" when the jury changes; the same texture image is "sporty" in the morning and "garish" in the afternoon. Any judgment that cannot be reduced to numbers and versions cannot enter an engineering process — only the meeting minutes.

EVOLUTION AI answers with a family of semantic interfaces: CLIP scores texture images on six style axes; the brand knowledge layer maps semantic vectors onto profiles of 5 brands and 19 car models; and BrandDNASchema writes "does this look like our brand" as executable constraint verdicts. The proposition of this chapter is: use interpretable semantic scoring to turn aesthetic judgment into regressable numbers. Algorithms will not replace taste, but aesthetic judgment must earn the same engineering standing as geometric tolerance — with a rubric, a version, and reproducibility.

2. Principles

Principle 1. Language never drives geometry directly; it must first become numbers and schemas

Before natural language or imagery enters the system, intent must be translated into objects with names, units, and bounds. A sentence like "a low, sporty SUV" is parsed by design_intent into structured parameters and parameter variants; a lamp texture is encoded by CLIP into six [0,1] scores. Once translation happens, judgment has a shape. Without translation, language is only atmosphere.

Principle 2. Fixed rubric, regressable scores

The entire value of a semantic score lies in regression: the same image, the same prompts, and the same weight version must yield the same score. Each of the platform's six style axes binds a Chinese label to a concrete English visual prompt — a deliberate prompt-engineering choice, because CLIP responds far better to concrete visual descriptions than to abstract adjectives. Prompts and the weight version (openai/clip-vit-base-patch32, about 605 MB) are archived and managed; only then do scores qualify to enter reviews and optimization loops.

Principle 3. Semantics must attach to nameable structure

"This car is sporty" is not executable. "Shoulder line rising, greenhouse line lowered, wheel arches flared" is. Semantics must hang on nameable structures: the regions eligible for texture are an explicit list (/texture/regions), brand constraints land on brand-gene items, and the feature lines (greenhouse, shoulder, waist, wheel arch, sill) form the styling skeleton. The more specific the attachment point, the more testable the semantics.

Principle 4. No data means NO_DATA, never a presumed pass

BrandDNASchema admits four verdicts: PASS, FAIL, NO_DATA, and N/A. NO_DATA is the most honest of them — when something cannot be measured now, it is labeled as such rather than waved through. When CLIP weights are missing, texture analysis falls back to geometric features and says "geometric features only" in the response. The first virtue of a semantic interface is not cleverness; it is refusing to pretend.

3. Case Study: From One Texture Image to a Brand Verdict

The semantic channel for texture. The texture module has only 3 endpoints: a region list, pattern analysis, and apply-to-session. A reference pattern entering /texture/analyze has geometric features and CLIP six-axis semantic features extracted in parallel; the two are fused and applied through /texture/apply/{session_id} to the target region of a given session — a lamp lens pattern, for example. When the semantic channel is unavailable, the system falls back to the geometric channel and labels it; the user always knows which kind of evidence they are holding.

The semantic mapping for brand. The brand knowledge layer maps the six-axis semantic vector onto brand profiles and recommended models, grounded in a knowledge base of 5 brands and 19 car models (backend/config/brand_design_knowledge.json, with the frontend preset mirror in src/config/carPresets.js). "Which brand does this image resemble" stops being small talk and becomes a reproducible vector comparison.

Contract verdicts for DNA. BrandDNASchema pins version 1.0, requires brand and on_brand_constraints at the top level, and converges rule types to four: zero, range, enum, and sealed. Every constraint is judged independently: zero forbids a feature outright, range defines a numeric corridor, enum restricts discrete values, and sealed freezes a value from change. Verdicts are limited to PASS, FAIL, NO_DATA, and N/A — aesthetic judgment thereby takes the same form as geometric tolerance: itemized, measurable, and rejectable.

One end-to-end confluence. A stylist supplies a lamp texture reference. CLIP scores it on the six style axes; brand knowledge recommends matching DNA and parameter adjustments; the Deliver page applies those adjustments inside an import-modify-export session and produces STEP output. Throughout, the designer can consult the local NURBS expert about a continuity warning. Semantics, parameters, and engineering delivery close the loop inside one system, and no step requires "paraphrasing by feel."

Cross-Domain Mapping.

  • Here = brand DNA is a structured contract that constrains texture and lamp-language generation. In RAG = the embedding model and chunking strategy need the same versioned contract, so swapping embeddings does not change the retrieval interface.
  • Here = semantic scoring returns an interpretable scalar with its basis, not a vibe. In LLM evaluation = semantic similarity must be pinned to a model version and threshold, or the scores are not comparable.

Capability status. Semantic scoring and the brand-DNA structure are [Implemented · Test-Locked] but depend on an external model download, placing them in the optional-capability class [Partially Implemented].

4. Anti-Patterns and Their Costs

Anti-pattern 1: The black-box composite score. One unexplainable number standing in for taste. The cost is a score that cannot be attributed or regressed, and reviews that degenerate into a shouting match of "trust the score or trust me."

Anti-pattern 2: Prompt drift. Style-axis prompts edited casually and never archived. The cost is that the same image scores differently this week than last, every historical verdict silently expires, and nobody can say when.

Anti-pattern 3: Language straight to geometry. Having a large model generate geometry directly from a sentence, skipping parameters and schemas. The cost is output that can be neither edited nor audited; every generation is like the first.

Anti-pattern 4: NO_DATA presumed PASS. Unmeasurable constraints waved through by default. The cost is a brand-verdict table stuffed with evidence-free consent — and the discovery, after something goes wrong, that the pass rate was always fictional.

5. Operational Checklist

  1. Give every semantic axis a Chinese label and a concrete English visual prompt; manage prompts in version control (following clip_semantics.py).
  2. Pin the weight version and loading path; when weights are missing, fall back to geometric features with an explicit label.
  3. Before semantic scores enter reviews, verify same-image-same-prompt-same-score, and encode regression cases in tests.
  4. Maintain an explicit list of texture-eligible regions; forbid the implicit convention that any region will do.
  5. Express brand constraints with the four rule types — zero, range, enum, sealed; anything beyond the four triggers a review of the rule design itself.
  6. Keep the NO_DATA verdict; every "cannot measure" goes on the record as itself.
  7. Route every natural-language entry through design_intent into parameters or variants; never connect it directly to geometry generation.
  8. Update the knowledge base (5 brands, 19 car models) and its frontend mirror together, avoiding dual-track facts.

6. Chapter Self-Check

  1. Can your "brand fit" say which constraints compose it and what each one returned? If not, it is still a slogan.
  2. Rerun last week's semantic scoring today — do the scores match? If not, on what grounds do they serve as review evidence?
  3. When CLIP weights are missing, does your interface honestly label "geometric features only" — or quietly produce results that look the same?

Chapter 11. The Testing Pyramid and the Thousand-Case Baseline

Author: Quantum Swordsman Volume IV · Engineering: Converging Uncertainty into Delivery

1. The Question

The most common misjudgement in AI-native systems is to treat a successful demonstration as a usable system. On a parametric styling platform, moving one slider for vehicle length changes the geometry kernel, the interface contract and the front-end rendering at the same time. A language model answering one specialist question correctly may fail the next. A demonstration proves that one path worked at one moment; testing must answer a different question: after anyone modifies any layer, does the system still keep the promises already made?

Two concrete questions follow. First, given a limited testing budget, at which layer should cases be placed so that the shortest feedback loop yields the strongest fault localisation? Second, how can a set of pass counts become reviewable evidence rather than a ritual of reassurance? This chapter takes the EVOLUTION AI platform's ~1,027-case baseline as its sample and discusses engineering answers to both.

2. Principles

First, price each layer separately instead of spreading effort evenly. The value and cost of tests follow a pyramid. Lower layers run faster, produce more stable results and localise failures more precisely; higher layers approach real user paths but are slower, more fragile and harder to diagnose. Geometric invariants, numerical boundaries and pure-function behaviour belong in algorithm unit tests. Cross-module fields and state machines belong in back-end integration tests. Component interaction and state synchronisation belong in front-end component tests. End-to-end coverage keeps a few main chains that prove the system still starts and completes one real piece of business.

Second, a baseline is a contract, and every number carries a date and a definition. The statement "roughly 1,027 cases pass" means something only when the layer breakdown, the execution commands, and the treatment of skips and expected failures are stated. Once a baseline enters a document it becomes an external promise; every later increase must be re-run and re-defined, never stitched from numbers of different dates.

Third, a red light matters more than a coverage figure. Coverage describes which code was executed; a gate answers whether a change may be merged, and one cannot substitute for the other. High coverage without blocking failures is expensive decoration; modest coverage with reliable red lights is a dependable foundation.

Fourth, every defect must leave a test behind. Each fix should add a case that fails reliably on the old code. The fix proves that this instance was corrected; the regression case proves it cannot return in the same form. A fix without a case is temporary first aid.

Old numbers are retained and labelled rather than erased—this is itself part of an auditable baseline.

Cross-Domain Mapping.

  • Here = algorithm, backend, and frontend suites run independently along the dependency direction, slow on top and fast at the bottom. In LLM evaluation = unit-level prompt assertions, link-level tool-call replays, and end-to-end baselines form the same shape; a lower-layer failure does not burn upper-layer budget.
  • Here = identical input yields an identical score, the baseline is pinned, and drift raises an alarm. In regressable LLM evaluation = decoding parameters and dataset version are fixed so every score change is attributable to a model or prompt change.

4. Anti-Patterns and Their Costs

The ice-cream cone. Pouring most of the budget into end-to-end tests looks closest to the user but produces long feedback loops and frequent random failures. A failed geometry assertion may originate in the proxy, authentication, serialisation or rendering. The team spends its time re-running and guessing instead of localising.

Coverage worship. Large amounts of code are executed to make a number look good, but effective assertions are missing. A passing test proves only that no exception was raised, not that the output is correct. Such a suite creates false confidence during refactoring.

Mocking everything. Unit layers replace the database, authentication and file system with mocks, so the integration layer disappears. Real failures tend to occur at boundaries: drifted field names, uncommitted transactions, a missing terminal state in a state machine.

Letting tests take the guest bypass. If the test environment inherits GUEST_MODE during the account-archiving period, every 401/403 case is silently admitted as a guest and the semantics of authorisation collapse. The platform therefore forces GUEST_MODE=false in backend/tests/conftest.py, making "tests must exercise real authentication" a hard constraint.

Number drift. A document says 489 while the code has reached roughly 1,027. The cost is not one wrong citation but the loss of credibility of the entire evidence system; afterwards every number is suspect.

Treating expected failure as success. xfail is an honest label, but it needs an owner and an exit condition. Left unattended, it becomes hidden debt that conceals a real capability gap.

5. Operational Checklist

  1. Fix three layer boundaries and commands: run python -m pytest tests/ -q under algorithm_model/, python -m pytest -q under backend/, and npx vitest run at the repository root; fixtures are not borrowed across layers.
  2. Make every command independently reproducible in a clean environment, without relying on a developer's residual state.
  3. Treat red as a block: the main branch must be fully green before merge; skips and expected failures are listed explicitly with reasons (e.g. RUN_REAL_LLM=1, the SISA migration).
  4. Record the baseline in a table: date, command, per-layer counts, skips and xfails; keep old rows when updating.
  5. Label historical snapshots with date and definition: 201 (9 Aug), 489 (27 Sep) and 483 (11 Oct) are snapshots and are never mixed with the current ~1,027.
  6. Prioritise contract tests: field names, enum values, state transitions and error codes need assertions, so that "it returned" never substitutes for "it returned correctly".
  7. Isolate the test environment: conftest.py forces guest mode and debug switches off, uses separate databases and temporary directories, and never touches production keys.
  8. Attach a regression case to every defect fix: the repair commit includes a test that fails on the old code.
  9. Classify warnings: register known warnings as owned, non-blocking items and review periodically; never silently convert a warning into a pass.
  10. Run the full suite periodically: update the baseline from that day's output, not from an earlier report.

6. Self-Check

  1. Can your baseline be reproduced in a clean environment with one command? If not, is the gap in environment isolation, data preparation or command packaging?
  2. In your last three defect fixes, how many left a regression case that fails on the old code? For those that did not, who carries the risk?
  3. Do all the test numbers you cite carry a date and a definition? When documents and code disagree, what rule does your team use to decide which is the fact?

Chapter 12. Observability and End-to-End Audit

Author: Quantum Swordsman Volume IV · Engineering: Converging Uncertainty into Delivery

1. The Question

Tests answer whether the system keeps its promises right now; observability answers an older question: which real execution stands behind that "pass" from three months ago? Who operated on which object at what time? When a quality score of 0.85 is challenged, can it be traced back to the evaluation that produced it, the parameters that fed it, the version that ran it? AI-native systems generate a large share of their output through models and algorithms, which are uncertain by nature. When a conclusion is questioned, a team cannot defend it with recollection and verbal assurance—only with records.

Two groups of concrete questions follow. First, what to record: recording everything drowns the signal and touches privacy; recording too little leaves no evidence at the critical moment. Second, how recording can avoid harming the system: if audit writes share the transaction and the exception path of the business flow, auditing itself becomes a source of failure. The proposition of this chapter is: every conclusion must be traceable to one real execution, and what supports that proposition is an audit data structure designed as a first-class citizen—not log-grepping as an afterthought.

2. Principles

First, facts land in tables, not scattered across text logs. Facts that can be questioned must be structured: who logged in, when, and by what method (login_records); which administrator performed which action on which object (admin_audit_logs); which visitor visited which page for how long (page_views). Structure means the data can be queried and aggregated by person, by object and by time window; plain text logs can only be searched by keyword and luck.

Second, auditing never blocks the business. Audit is a side channel of evidence, not a link in the main flow. When an audit write fails, the business must continue. Making audit a synchronous hard dependency lets the observer kidnap the observed.

Third, record metadata only, never content. Security events carry IP, path, counts and the names of matched signatures; they never carry prompt bodies, response content or sensitive user data. The value of audit lies in "what happened", not "what was said"—once the latter is stored, it becomes a new attack surface of its own.

Fourth, every number carries a date and a definition. The observability system must observe itself: every externally cited metric is labelled with its snapshot time and statistical definition; old and new snapshots are kept side by side and never overwrite each other.

3. Anatomy of the Case

Among the platform's 17 ORM tables, three exist purely for audit and observation. login_records (LoginRecord) stores the sign-in ledger: time, method (password, WeChat scan, official-account authorisation) and IP. admin_audit_logs (AdminAuditLog) stores administrative operations: operator, action and object. page_views (PageView) stores page-visit telemetry: path, dwell time and visitor identity. The three are not one universal log table; they are modelled separately according to how each will be questioned: security questions look up sign-ins by person, compliance questions look up operations by object, and product questions look up traffic by page.

The read side is equally structured. The analytics.py module exposes 21 endpoints for telemetry statistics—PV/UV, daily/weekly/monthly trends, mean dwell time and page distribution—all aggregated from page_views. The admin.py module exposes 7 endpoints that surface user management, login records, audit logs and back-end errors to the admin console. The standalone /admin/ console keeps five tabs—login records, user management, back-end errors, audit logs and impact evidence—so that "who did what in the system" becomes a clickable fact rather than a SQL statement an operator must run by hand.

Beyond the business tables, the platform runs a dedicated security-event stream. backend/app/fence/audit.py writes security events as JSONL to backend/logs/security-audit.jsonl, rotated by a RotatingFileHandler. Event names follow a "domain.object.result" convention—auth.login.failure (brute-force early warning), authz.denied (privilege-escalation attempts), ratelimit.hit (rate limiting triggered), llm.request (LLM proxy call metadata), llm.guard.injection (prompt-injection signature matched), and so on. Every record carries the request_id filled in by the request-context middleware, so one request can be cross-identified between business logs and the security-event stream. Two design details embody the principles of this section: emit() promises never to raise, so an audit failure degrades to one warning log line; and the event payload accepts metadata only—prompt bodies and keys never reach the disk.

The final link of the audit system is its own credibility. The audit report of 10 August 2026 labels "201 cases, all passing" as the baseline of 9 August and keeps it unaltered. The supplementary audit of 11 October 2026 opens a new snapshot: 483 items (341 back-end passes, one skip, two expected failures, 142 front-end passes) plus the merged commit range. Both sets of numbers coexist in the same document, and a legend explicitly forbids either from overwriting the other—the observability system applies the same definitional discipline to its own figures.

Cross-Domain Mapping.

  • Here = every restyle, training run, and adjudication leaves a JSONL audit record, so the decision chain can be replayed. In a general agent = tool-call traces, intermediate reasoning, and the final action are all retained; incident review does not rely on memory.
  • Here = metrics and logs answer "which layer failed". In RAG = retrieval hits, cited sources, and generated passages must align line by line, so a hallucination can be traced to retrieval or to generation.

Capability status. JSONL audit trails and request tracing are [Implemented · Test-Locked]; cross-service causal attribution remains a design principle [Design Principle].

4. Anti-Patterns and Their Costs

Treating logs as audit. Scattered print statements and text logs are called an audit system. The cost appears at questioning time: aggregation by person or object is impossible, and grep-produced fragments form neither a chain nor evidence.

Letting audit block the business. Audit writes share the business transaction, so a database wobble kills the main flow. The observer kidnaps the observed, and the team eventually switches auditing off "to stay safe"—the most expensive outcome of all.

Recording everything, including content. Prompt bodies, responses and even keys are written to logs. Convenient for debugging in the short term; in the long term it copies sensitive data to the least-defended location, and the audit store itself becomes the target of exfiltration.

Recording success but not failure. Only successful logins are persisted; auth.login.failure is missing. Brute-force attempts are completely invisible until an account falls, and the retrospective finds no door to look through.

Numbers without definitions. A report says "all green" without a date, and 201 is conflated with 483. Once the observability system's own numbers lose credibility, its observations of everything else lose authority too.

5. Operational Checklist

  1. Administrative actions land in admin_audit_logs: operator, action and object are all present; follow the assertion style of test_admin_rbac.py for set_active and reset_password.
  2. Sign-in events land in login_records: time, method and IP at minimum; failures are recorded as faithfully as successes.
  3. Page telemetry lands in page_views: path, dwell time and visitor_id; aggregate queries read this table only, never the business tables.
  4. Security events go through emit() in backend/app/fence/audit.py; event names follow the "domain.object.result" convention.
  5. Audit writes never raise: every new audit point stays a side channel, degrading to a warning log on failure.
  6. Payloads carry metadata only: IP, path, counts, signature names; prompt bodies, keys and response content are forbidden in audit.
  7. Thread request_id through business logs and the security-event stream so one request can be identified at both ends.
  8. Audit reading happens in the standalone /admin/ console; the public front end contains no admin code or routes.
  9. Write tests for auditing: after an administrative operation, assert the corresponding row exists in the audit table—do not stop at a 200 response.
  10. Metrics cited in reports carry snapshot dates and definitions; old and new snapshots are kept side by side and never overwrite each other.

6. Self-Check

  1. When a quality conclusion is challenged, can you locate the execution, the parameters and the version that produced it within ten minutes? If not, at which layer does the chain break?
  2. When an audit write fails in your system, what happens to the business flow? Was that answer designed, or has it never been asked?
  3. Does your security-event stream contain failure records such as auth.login.failure? What trace would a brute-force attempt leave in your system today?

Chapter 13. Security Guardrails and Guest Mode

Author: Quantum Swordsman Volume IV · Engineering: Converging Uncertainty into Delivery

1. The Question

An AI-native platform has a wider attack surface than a traditional web application. It adds three exposures that traditional systems do not have: the model proxy forwards requests to external LLMs, and a user-controllable URL is a ready-made SSRF channel; users entrust the platform with their API keys for various providers, and the storage format decides the loss when the database is dumped; and for demonstration and acquisition the platform wants anonymous visitors to experience full functionality—between "open" and "breached" there is a single switch. Security design here does not answer "whether to add locks" but three finer questions: what the default posture is, when a violation is discovered, and how openness is explicitly designed.

The proposition of this chapter is: deny by default, admit explicitly, and fail fast in production. Deny-by-default converges the attack surface into whitelists; explicit admission gives every opening a name that can be audited; fail-fast moves security violations from "run-time incident" to "start-up error"—better to refuse to boot than to go live with a weakness.

2. Principles

First, deny by default; every admission is a whitelist. A production CORS whitelist, a fixed provider registry and a strict CSP share one structure: assume the requester is hostile, then list the admitted names one by one. Outside the whitelist there is no "temporary pass".

Second, fail fast in production; violations stop at start-up. Security configuration is validated when the application boots, not when it is first attacked. A failed validation raises immediately and the process refuses to come up. A service that cannot start is an incident; a service running with a weak secret is a catastrophe.

Third, layer the secrets; each has an independent blast radius. The platform's own signing key, the provider keys entrusted by users, and the deployment-side tunnel credentials are three separate secrets: stored separately, rotated separately, and the compromise of one does not cascade.

Fourth, openness is explicit design, not a permissions bug. Guest mode—a site-wide pass—must be a product decision with guardrails: its boundary is written into code, into tests and into start-up validation, not left as a temporary change nobody remembers.

3. Anatomy of the Case

Start with where fail-fast lands. The settings model in backend/app/config.py carries a production validator: when ENVIRONMENT=production, SECRET_KEY must not equal the built-in development default and must be at least 32 characters; DEBUG must be false (with debug on, CORS degenerates to any origin and 500 responses expose stack traces); GUEST_MODE must be off. Any violation makes pydantic raise a ValidationError at start-up and the application refuses to go live. The deployment side agrees: deploy/docker-compose.yml uses ${SECRET_KEY:?...} so that a missing secret fails the compose run directly—two gates, one outside and one inside.

Now the layering of secrets. JWTs are signed with PyJWT and passwords are stored hashed; user-entrusted provider API keys enter the api_keys table encrypted with Fernet, the encryption key derived from SECRET_KEY via SHA256—plaintext keys exist only briefly in the memory that forwards a request. Deployment credentials such as the cpolar authtoken live only in .env and are forbidden inside command payloads. The three layers contain one another nowhere, and the loss of any one does not automatically surrender the other two.

Guest mode is the specimen of explicit openness. With GUEST_MODE enabled during the account-archiving period, get_current_user in security.py resolves token-less requests to a built-in guest account (created lazily and idempotently), so the whole site is reachable by anonymous visitors. But this is not unbounded exposure: enabling it in production triggers the fail-fast above; administrative semantics still converge through dependency injection; and on the test side backend/tests/conftest.py forces GUEST_MODE=false, guaranteeing that 401/403 cases exercise the real authentication path. Two cases in test_guest_mode.py lock the boundary: guest admission works, and enabling it in production must fail.

CSP shows how deny-by-default is refined to path granularity. The security-headers middleware in main.py uniformly injects X-Content-Type-Options, X-Frame-Options, CSP, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy and Cache-Control, and strips server: uvicorn. The default CSP is strict, with script-src 'self'. The standalone admin console, however, depends on vue-i18n's runtime compilation and needs unsafe-eval. The platform's answer is not a site-wide relaxation but a path-selected policy: only the /admin document admits unsafe-eval, while the public API and the main site stay strict. test_admin_console.py turns that boundary into an assertion: unsafe-eval appears in, and only in, the admin document's CSP.

SSRF is solved by eliminating choice. llm_proxy.py hard-codes the official endpoints of seven providers in a registry; users select a provider but can never submit a URL. Request bodies are additionally fenced (50MB globally, 256KB per LLM request, 64 messages, 60,000 characters), and the prompt-injection signature scan records warnings by default. The proxy passes bytes through, but the destination is never decided by the requester.

Cross-Domain Mapping.

  • Here = four authentication tiers (public, optional, required, admin) are enforced in one dependency-injection layer, defaulting to deny. In RAG = document permissions must be filtered before retrieval, not scrubbed out of the answer afterwards.
  • Here = guest mode allows reads and blocks writes. In a general agent = tool allowlists are granted per session, and unauthorized tools report unavailability truthfully.

Capability status. The four auth tiers, guest mode, and default-deny posture are [Implemented · Test-Locked] (152 routed endpoints split 114 / 9 / 12 / 17); threat modeling and key rotation are engineering discipline [Design Principle].

4. Anti-Patterns and Their Costs

Going live on a weak default secret. SECRET_KEY keeps the development default, so JWT signing and user-key encryption fall together. The opposite of fail-fast is not "it boots" but "it runs naked in silence with a predictable secret".

Guest mode reaching production. A demo configuration is carried into deployment and every protected endpoint—including super-admin ones—opens to anonymous visitors. The cost is not merely being attacked: when it happens, no record can tell a visitor from an administrator.

User-controllable URLs. The model proxy accepts an endpoint field from the front end. This is textbook SSRF: internal addresses and cloud metadata endpoints are exposed at once. Eliminating the field beats any filtering regex.

One-size-fits-all CSP. Either the whole site gets unsafe-eval (no CSP in effect) or the whole site is strict (the console goes blank). The correct granularity is path scoping, locked by tests.

Tests taking the guest bypass. The test environment inherits GUEST_MODE=true, every 401/403 case is admitted, and authentication regression silently dies. One forced-off line in conftest is worth more than a hundred cases.

Secrets inside command payloads. Authtokens are written into start-up scripts and command lines. Process lists and shell history are leak surfaces; credentials belong in .env and environment variables only.

5. Operational Checklist

  1. Before a production launch, validate three hard conditions: SECRET_KEY is non-default and at least 32 characters, DEBUG=false, GUEST_MODE=false (see the validator in config.py).
  2. On the deployment side, use ${SECRET_KEY:?...} so compose fails on a missing secret, forming a double gate with the application validator.
  3. User API keys always enter the api_keys table Fernet-encrypted; plaintext never reaches the database or the logs.
  4. For every new outbound call, ask "who decided the URL": it must come from a server-side registry; requesters never supply destinations.
  5. CSP changes are allowed only as path-scoped relaxations, following the /admin pattern, with a regression assertion for each new scope.
  6. Security headers are injected uniformly by middleware; ad-hoc per-route rewrites are forbidden.
  7. Production CORS whitelists origins explicitly; the combination of ACAO * with credentials is forbidden.
  8. The test environment forces guest mode and debug switches off in conftest; authorisation cases must take the real path.
  9. Give every security boundary at least one test: guest admission, production fail-fast and CSP scoping each have theirs.
  10. Credentials live only in .env; review scripts and documents before committing so that no secret enters command payloads or git history.

6. Self-Check

  1. If the production SECRET_KEY were replaced by the development default, would your system refuse to start—or run silently until breached?
  2. Does your "open experience" switch have all three locks: forbidden in production, administration still converged, and tests forced onto real authentication? Which one is missing?
  3. If an attacker obtained every encrypted user API key in your system, what else would they still need? Is that "something else" exactly one secret?

Chapter 14. Build, Deploy and Release

Author: Quantum Swordsman Volume IV · Engineering: Converging Uncertainty into Delivery

1. The Question

Releasing an AI-native platform is far more than "package and upload". The algorithm layer carries a Cython extension that must be compiled; the front end drags along a heavy 3D library like Three.js; the back end must pass secret validation before it boots; and the knowledge-base documents have to ship with the site. Two engineering questions follow. First, why does the build output look the way it does? When dist/ contains nine chunks, each chunk should exist for a reason, not as the bundler's improvisation. Second, can the deployment topology be reproduced on a fresh machine? If going live depends on seven manual steps in one engineer's memory, every release is an unauditable performance.

The proposition of this chapter is: build output that can be explained, deployment topology that can be reproduced. Explainable means the output structure corresponds one-to-one with declared intent; reproducible means the topology lives entirely in declarative files, comes up with one command, is gated by health checks, and receives its secrets only from the environment.

2. Principles

First, the output is the documentation. Every rule in the build configuration is a recorded decision: why this large library is cached whole, why that small one gets its own chunk. Reading the configuration should reconstruct the intent; reading the output should verify it.

Second, the topology is declarative and start-up is a single command. How many services exist, who depends on whom, and which volume holds the data are all declared in compose files. The first deployment and the hundredth run the same command and produce the same result.

Third, secrets are separated from artefacts. Images and static bundles never carry secrets; secrets are injected by the environment at run time, and a missing secret fails fast. What may be public is thoroughly public; what must stay secret never enters a build.

Fourth, minimise the published surface. The public site carries only public functions; no admin code enters the public bundle—routes, menus and chunks are all removed, and the console is hosted separately by the back end.

3. Anatomy of the Case

Start with the build side. The manualChunks function in vite.config.js is a decision list with comments: @element-plus/icons-vue is matched before element-plus—because the latter's id contains the former's substring, and with the wrong order the icon library would be merged into the wrong chunk. Three.js splits into a three core and a three-examples add-on chunk, cached separately; vue, markdown-it and axios each stand alone. The comments also record the counter-decision: "large libraries are deliberately cached whole rather than split without limit—over-splitting multiplies the request count and destroys library-level cache benefits", which is why chunkSizeWarningLimit is raised to 1000kB to silence meaningless size warnings. Two further release-side decisions: base: './' uses relative paths to keep the bundle portable, and sourcemap: false limits the information exposed in public artefacts. The docsPlugin copies the Markdown, PDF, HTML and SVG files under docs/ into dist/docs/ at build time, so the knowledge base ships with the site at the same version as the code—documentation-as-product in its delivery form.

On the deployment side the repository keeps two complementary topologies. The root Dockerfile is the all-in-one form: a multi-stage build compiles the Cython extension of the NURBS kernel in the builder stage; the run stage executes as a non-root user (uid 1000), declares the /app/data and /app/logs volumes, and health-checks /health. The root docker-compose.yml orchestrates it into a four-service stack—backend, PostgreSQL 16, Redis 7 (with requirepass enforced) and nginx; the database exposes no 5432 to the outside by default, and start-up order converges through depends_on: service_healthy. The deploy/ directory holds the split form: backend.Dockerfile (python:3.11-slim, a non-root appuser, the start.py entry, health-checking /api/v1/health) and frontend.Dockerfile (npm ci then vite build on node:20-alpine, artefacts handed to nginx:1.27-alpine) build separately; deploy/docker-compose.yml exposes only port 80 to the outside, declares SECRET_KEY as required via ${SECRET_KEY:?...}, and keeps Redis as an optional, commented-out service.

The last mile of the traffic entry is pinned down by the nginx configuration. deploy/nginx.conf turns off server_tokens, aligns client_max_body_size 64m with the back end's 50MB upload limit, and enables gzip for JSON and static assets. default.conf.template writes the routing as five blocks: /api/v1, /docs and /openapi.json proxy to the back end while forwarding X-Request-ID and the real IP (echoing the audit chain of Chapter 12); /admin proxies separately to the back-end-hosted console; hashed /assets/ and images are cached for a year; unknown paths fall back to index.html for the SPA; and dotfiles (.env, .git) are denied outright. The minimised published surface shows in the division of labour: the gh-pages static site contains no admin code at all, and the admin console is hosted independently by the back end's StaticFiles under /admin/, with the login gate enforced server-side.

The first-deployment command chain is therefore short: npm run build produces dist/; docker compose up -d --build raises the whole stack; a fresh database takes one alembic upgrade head; and legacy SQLite data, if needed, is migrated once by backend/scripts/migrate_sqlite_to_pg.py. Local development is another declared topology—Vite on 5173, FastAPI on 8000 and a cpolar tunnel on 4040—while the LLM inference service is orchestrated separately in docker-compose.llm.yml, never holding the main stack hostage.

Cross-Domain Mapping.

  • Here = frontend bundle, backend image, and algorithm package are version-locked together, and rollback is atomic. In an LLM application = model version, prompt version, and evaluation baseline must ship together, or a rollback cannot reproduce the outputs of that moment.
  • Here = build chunking is decoupled from deployment topology; development and production differ only in one access layer. In an agent platform = the capability list ships with the version and is externally checkable, so "the docs say it exists" never outruns production.

Capability status. Build chunking, images, and deployment topology are [Implemented · Test-Locked]; canary release and automated rollback are roadmap items [Partially Implemented].

4. Anti-Patterns and Their Costs

Splitting chunks without limit. Worshipping "smaller is better" and giving every dependency its own file multiplies the request count, destroys library-level caching, and makes the first screen slower. Size-warning-driven splitting is vanity engineering.

Baking secrets into images. Writing SECRET_KEY into a Dockerfile or static configuration leaks it the moment the image is pushed, and rotation then requires rebuilding the artefact. Secrets belong to run time, not build time.

Exposing the database to the public network. Mapping 5432 to all host interfaces "for debugging". The commented-out port mapping in compose is a design: open it temporarily when needed and close it after—never leave it open by default.

Shipping admin code in the public site. Admin routes, menus and chunks distributed with the public bundle. An attacker does not need to guess the console address; they can download the source and study it at leisure. Every extra measure of published surface thins the defence.

Orchestration without health checks. Treating a dependency container's "started" as "ready" sends traffic to a process still initialising. depends_on with service_healthy is not ceremony; it is the only defence against start-up ordering faults.

Documents drifting from artefacts. The site goes live while the knowledge base stays a version behind. Documents must enter dist/ with the build, shipping and rolling back together with the code.

5. Operational Checklist

  1. Build with npm run build; when changing manualChunks, update the decision rationale in the comments at the same time.
  2. Audit the chunk list in dist/: every large file needs a stated owner and reason—merge or delete what cannot be explained.
  3. First deployment: docker compose up -d --build, then docker compose exec backend bash -c "cd /app/backend && alembic upgrade head".
  4. Generate secrets with python -c "import secrets; print(secrets.token_urlsafe(48))"; they go only into .env, never into a Dockerfile or a command payload.
  5. After start-up, verify health checks—the back end's /api/v1/health and the nginx probes—before opening traffic.
  6. nginx exposes only 80/443; database and Redis ports are unmapped by default, and temporary debugging binds 127.0.0.1 only.
  7. Align upload limits at both ends: client_max_body_size and the back end's MAX_FILE_SIZE share one definition.
  8. Before publishing the public bundle, verify zero admin code: no /admin route, no admin chunk, no console entry.
  9. Put the data volumes (pg-data, evoai-data) under a backup plan; containers are disposable, volumes are not.
  10. Rehearse rollback together with release: roll the static site back by image tag, and keep database changes on reversible migrations.

6. Self-Check

  1. Open your build output: can the reason for every chunk be found in the configuration comments? Are the unexplained ones intent or accident?
  2. Given a fresh machine, a repository and one .env, can a single command reproduce the whole topology? Which manual steps remain undeclared?
  3. Does your public artefact contain code or secrets that should not be there? What check proves "it does not", instead of believing "it does not"?

Chapter 15 · The General Design Department: A Human–Machine Hall for Deliberation

Author: Quantum Swordsman (量子剑客) Volume V · Organization: how humans and machines divide labor Core proposition: machines supply candidates and evidence; humans retain judgment and accountability.

1. Introduction: when generation becomes cheap, judgment becomes the bottleneck

Automotive styling is constrained simultaneously by market context, brand DNA, engineering hard points, manufacturing processes, and shifting cultural taste. It is a textbook open complex giant system. No single role can produce a global answer: a designer can tell whether a proposal "looks like this brand," an engineer can tell whether it can be built, and a model can enumerate thousands of parameter sets overnight. Yet none of them can individually bear the responsibility for declaring "this is the final version." Solving a complex problem is first an organizational problem and only second a tooling problem.

Once AI enters development, teams tend to fall into one of two extremes. The first is instrumentalization: the process stays untouched, a generate button is attached at the end, and AI becomes a faster drawing pencil while complexity remains ungoverned. The second is outsourcing: aesthetic and brand judgment is delegated to a composite score, the team reads only the ranking, and the search converges on proposals that score well but no longer resemble a car. The former changes nothing about the organization; the latter abandons accountability. Neither is AI-native.

The general design department and the metasynthesis hall proposed by Qian Xuesen offer a third form: an overall plan governs the subsystems, a triangular loop of expert system, machine system, and knowledge system handles complex problems, and the principle of "human–machine combination with humans in the lead" is preserved. This chapter asks how that organizational form becomes a running system rather than a management slogan.

Boundary statement. The "metasynthesis seminar hall" and the "general design department" described here are organizational discipline and design principles [Design Principle], not a module inside the platform. What the codebase carries today is their engineering shadow: eight frozen hard parameters, fixed thresholds (G0 0.1 mm / G1 1.0°), JSONL audit trails, and the separation of advice from adjudication. There is no automated seminar-hall module; reading it as a shipped feature is a misreading.

2. Principles

2.1 Establish the whole first: fix interfaces before parallel implementation

The first duty of a general design department is overall planning, not doing the subsystems' work for them. Operationally, this means freezing the layering, boundaries, and interface contracts before allowing modules to evolve independently. The department does not write algorithms, but it decides the shape in which algorithms are called. Once an interface becomes a contract, subsystem schedules stop holding each other hostage; once an interface stays vague, even strong local capabilities cancel each other out during integration.

2.2 Three-system division: experts qualify, machines quantify, knowledge remembers

The expert system frames the question, sets boundaries, and makes value judgments. The machine system performs massive exploration, precise computation, and pattern recognition. The knowledge system stores, retrieves, and activates experience. These are not three voters in parallel but three consecutive responsibilities: qualitative judgment defines the shape of the search space, quantitative search finds solutions inside that shape, and knowledge turns each solution into a prior for the next round. Remove any corner and the loop degenerates into an open line.

2.3 Separate recommendation from adjudication

A machine may provide candidates, evidence, and confidence, but it cannot sign on a human's behalf. Every proposal that enters delivery must have a named adjudicator and a traceable evidence chain. What is separated is not authority but the attribution of error: a bad recommendation can be corrected, while a bad adjudication must be owned. This discipline determines whether the system can be repaired when it fails.

2.4 Report absence honestly

Machine capability has limits. When a dependency is missing, the system should explicitly return unavailability or a degraded marker rather than filling the interface with simulated data. Trust can only rest on honesty; one fabricated "machine opinion" is enough to discredit every recommendation that follows. Honest degradation is not a capability defect; it is part of the system's credibility.

3. Case study: how the EVOLUTION AI hall actually runs

3.1 The general design department: thin-shell orchestration and an independent algorithm package

The backend consists of 19 route modules and 154 HTTP endpoints (152 route endpoints plus 2 system endpoints). It connects downward to 17 ORM tables and upward to 12 frontend pages. What matters is not the count but the division of labor: algorithm_model is an independently installable Python package with its own entry points, dependencies, and tests. The backend performs parameter validation, authentication, persistence, and orchestration; it does not reimplement geometry.

This is the technical expression of a general design department. The orchestration layer does not manufacture parts; it defines the assembly contract between them. The algorithm package can run, be tested, and be replaced without the web layer. When the geometry kernel is upgraded, the external semantics of all 154 endpoints remain unchanged; when a page is revised, the algorithm package is untouched. The payoff of establishing the whole first is precisely this non-hostage-taking mode of evolution.

3.2 The expert system: thresholds are defined by people

The continuity thresholds G1 5° and G2 2°, together with the A/B/C/D cutoffs of 0.85, 0.70, and 0.50, were not learned by a model. They encode a consensus among surface engineers. The platform turns them into executable criteria so that every evaluation uses the same ruler—but the graduations on that ruler are always set by people. The NURBS expert dialogue follows the same logic: it supplies domain knowledge and retrieval cues, while the choice of proposal and the tolerance for deviation remain with designers and quality engineers.

3.3 The machine system: exploring inside a human frame

A Bayesian session searches a 14-dimensional space that people carve out of the 22-dimensional CarParams; dimensions carrying sizes, regulatory constraints, and structural hard points are frozen. The acquisition function (EI or UCB) answers only "where should the next parameter set be tried," never "which design is more beautiful." After scores are fed back, the Gaussian-process surrogate updates its belief; the loop uses the EI minimum improvement 0.01 (ei_xi) as its stopping reference, and the plateau—typically reached within 20 to 40 rounds—is confirmed by session observation and human judgment. The machine performs rational exploration; value judgment stays on the human side.

3.4 The knowledge system: turning the last round into the next prior

The samples exported after each session are the fossil record of every qualitative-to-quantitative transition. They can enter a training task and become input to the next model, or enter the knowledge base and become a reference for the next review. The purpose of the knowledge system is not archiving but shortening the distance to a valid solution next time. The platform organizes more than twelve documents into a four-layer, three-dimensional knowledge graph so that knowledge appears at the moment it must be mobilized rather than sleeping in a directory.

Chapter 16 · Documentation as Product: The Knowledge Base and Consistency Auditing

Author: Quantum Swordsman (量子剑客) Volume V · Organization: how humans and machines divide labor Core proposition: documentation must be verified against the same source as the code; documentation drift is forbidden.

1. Introduction: when documents become inputs to the system

Traditional teams treat documentation as an accessory to delivery: the code is written first, the docs are patched in later, and human memory holds the two together. In AI-native development this arrangement fails outright, for two reasons. First, AI agents are a new class of reader—they consult the README, the architecture document, and the API reference to decide how to modify code. A drifted document no longer merely misleads human readers; it becomes a wrong instruction to an agent, amplifying deviation at industrial speed. Second, every commitment a complex system makes lives in its documents: endpoint counts, table structures, threshold conventions, test baselines. Once documents detach from the source, commitments can no longer be checked, and the team's trust in the system dissolves.

Documentation drift rarely comes from malice; it comes from inertia. Changing one line of code takes a minute, synchronizing five documents takes half an hour, so the debt is deferred. The bill arrives during integration, reviews, and the onboarding of new members. This chapter's position is simple: documentation is not the shadow of code but a product of the same rank. It has owners, acceptance criteria, automated auditing—and it is verified against the same source as the code.

2. Principles

2.1 Documentation is a product

Every document should have a named owner, an intended audience, and acceptance criteria; it is reviewed and changed together with the code. A documentation "defect" can be defined as concretely as a code defect: a number that disagrees with the source, a dead link, an unlabeled counting convention, a promised capability that does not exist. Only what can be defined as a defect can be filed as a defect—and only then does documentation have quality.

2.2 The source code is the single source of truth

Facts of scale—endpoint counts, table counts, page counts, parameter dimensions, test counts—are allowed exactly one origin: the current codebase. Every number in a document should be able to answer "which piece of source or which command produced it." Documents may organize, explain, and narrate; they may not invent facts. When a disagreement is found, the document is corrected to match the source, never the reverse.

2.3 Mechanical checking first, judgment second

Whatever a script can check should not be left to the human eye. Counting, dead links, and missing navigation entries are mechanical work and belong to a deterministic script. The script extracts facts only; "how this should be phrased" remains a human judgment. The division of labor in documentation mirrors that in design: machines enumerate and compare; humans qualify and adjudicate.

2.4 History is not rewritten; conventions are always labeled

Historical reports are archives, not living documents. Old numbers are preserved with their dates and counting conventions; new baselines start a new line. It is forbidden to splice numbers from different dates into a single conclusion, and forbidden to retroactively edit the facts inside a historical report. The object of an audit is "whether current documents agree with reality," not "whether history looks tidy."

3. Case study: how one-click auditing actually works

The platform turns documentation consistency into a repeatable skill (doc-code-audit). When triggered, it first runs a deterministic script built only on the standard library to extract a factual baseline from the source: the total number of HTTP endpoints (the APIRouter variables across route modules plus the system endpoints in main.py), the number of ORM tables (the __tablename__ entries in database.py), the number of frontend pages (route records in router.js), the number of CarParams fields, and the dimensionality of the default Bayesian search space. It then checks every statement of scale in the documents against that baseline, correcting documents to match the source wherever they disagree. Dead links must be fixed to paths verified to exist—guessing is forbidden; file:/// machine-local links are rewritten as repository-relative paths; and the script is re-run until it reports zero problems. If the session also involves code changes, the three test layers are executed for real and their actual output is used as evidence.

This mechanism guards a factual baseline shared across the entire repository: 19 route modules, 154 HTTP endpoints, 17 ORM tables, 12 frontend pages, and roughly 1,027 automated tests (200 algorithm, 411 backend, 416 frontend). The architecture document declares at the top that "all modules, paths, and data in this document take the current codebase as the sole source of truth," labeled with the corresponding version and date. The evolution of the test baseline is likewise traceable: 201 cases on 2026-08-09, 489 on 2026-09-27, 483 items on 2026-10-11, and roughly 1,027 as the current baseline (2026-10-25, per docs/VALIDATION_REPORT_20261025.md)—five figures, each with its own date and convention, and every citation must name its time point. The endpoint counting convention was unified from the historical 118 to the current 154 (152 route endpoints plus 2 system endpoints); the old number is preserved and labeled rather than erased from the report.

Cross-Domain Mapping.

  • Here = docs live in the same repository as code and are reconciled within the same commit; a stale number counts as a defect. In a RAG knowledge base = documents and index must be rebuilt together; a lagging index is a wrong answer.
  • Here = the glossary and chapters cite each other, with one source for every figure. In LLM evaluation = the evaluation spec and the scoring script share one origin, so "the metric in the doc" and "the metric in the script" never diverge.

Promising nonexistent capabilities The whitepaper describes interfaces not yet implemented External commitments cannot be honored; reviews lose their baseline

5. Operational checklist

  1. Does every document have a named owner, and does its header state the corresponding version or date?
  2. For every number of scale in a document, can you point to its source-code origin or generating command?
  3. Is there a deterministic script that automatically checks baseline facts such as endpoint, table, and page counts?
  4. Is the dead-link count zero, with every fix verified against a real target first?
  5. Are historical snapshots labeled with date and counting convention, and left unedited in their factual bodies?
  6. Are document changes reviewed and committed together with the corresponding code changes?
  7. When an audit finds a problem, is the document corrected against the source and the verification re-run until the script reports zero issues?
  8. Are key figures—such as the ~1,027-test baseline—consistent across all current documents?

Chapter 17 · Roles and Rhythms of the AI-Native Team

Author: Quantum Swordsman (量子剑客) Volume V · Organization: how humans and machines divide labor Core proposition: six roles, three rhythms, one acceptance line.

1. Introduction: roles are not a staffing table but the accountability structure of documents

The most common way to discuss an AI-native team is to ask "how many people, and what kind, do we still need?" That question is framed incorrectly from the start. Organizational design for an AI-native team should not begin with headcount; it should begin with the system's commitment structure. Every class of commitment the platform makes to the outside—design philosophy, development method, system architecture, interface contracts, quality standards, validation evidence—must have a named guardian. Commitments are written in documents, and documents live on top of the code; therefore roles can be derived in reverse from the documentation system. This repository's knowledge base happens to consist of six classes of documents: philosophy, method, architecture, API, quality, and validation. Six classes of documents imply six classes of roles. This is no coincidence but the organizational corollary of "documentation as product."

Only with roles in place does rhythm become meaningful. AI raises execution speed by an order of magnitude; if the rhythm of decision and acceptance is not redesigned alongside, the team is dragged under by the machine's pace: reviews become ceremonies, changes go unsedimented, and the noise of fast variables drowns the direction of the order parameters. This chapter adds the time dimension to roles: three rhythms and one acceptance line.

2. Principles

2.1 Derive roles from commitments, not from skills

First enumerate the classes of commitment the system makes; then appoint a guardian for each class. Skills may be shared across people; accountability may not be left vacant. One person may wear two hats, but at any moment each hat sits on exactly one head.

2.2 Six roles, each guarding one class of document

The philosophy keeper guards the design philosophy, meta-theory, and systems-thinking documents, adjudicating "does this accord with our design view." The method engineer guards the methodology document and maintains the development pipeline and its stage gates. The chief architect guards the architecture design document and freezes layering and boundaries. The contract steward guards the API reference and keeps fields and endpoints one hundred percent aligned. The quality engineer guards the quality standards and the test baseline, defining criteria and watching over the pyramid. The validation lead guards the validation report and the factual baseline, signing every release. A role's authority derives from the document it guards, and a document's force derives from its guardian; the two are each other's credential.

2.3 Three rhythms run on separate levels; never cross the frequencies

The round-level rhythm (seconds to hours) belongs to the machine: single-round Bayesian suggestions, red tests, build feedback—closed automatically, with humans intervening only on exceptions. The iteration rhythm (one to two weeks) belongs to the team: design reviews, variant adjudication, document synchronization, defect retrospectives, all run on a fixed agenda. The evolution rhythm (months to quarters) belongs to the whole: threshold adjustments, baseline updates, knowledge-system revisions, and introspection of the program itself, which must follow the knowledge-layer process. Rewriting an order parameter at round-level speed, or handling a red test with evolution-level composure, are both frequency crossings.

2.4 One acceptance line, one ruler for everyone

Whatever the role and whatever the rhythm, every output crosses the same acceptance line before delivery: all automated tests pass, the documentation consistency audit reports zero issues, and key decisions carry an evidence chain. The line does not bend for seniority, deadlines, or "this change is tiny." Only with a single ruler does a team share a single language of quality.

3. Case study: where the six roles land in EVOLUTION AI

Mapping the six roles onto the platform, each has a concrete object of guardianship and a concrete acceptance artifact. The philosophy keeper guards the design philosophy, design meta-theory, and systems-thinking documents; any adjustment to the quality thresholds—G1 5°, G2 2°, and the A/B/C/D cutoffs—must pass through their adjudication, because a threshold is not a parameter but an encoding of the design view. The method engineer guards the methodology document; adding or removing stages and gates in the five-stage pipeline is their remit. The chief architect guards the architecture design document and maintains the layered structure of 19 route modules, 17 ORM tables, and 12 pages, together with its boundary statements; seating any new module requires an architecture review first. The contract steward guards the API reference: the fields, error codes, and state machines of all 154 endpoints (152 route plus 2 system) must align one hundred percent between document and implementation. The quality engineer guards the ~1,027-test baseline (200 algorithm, 411 backend, 416 frontend); skips and expected failures must be listed explicitly with reasons. The validation lead guards the validation report and factual baseline, re-running the three test layers and the consistency script before every release and signing on the real output.

The three rhythms likewise have empirical footing. Round level: a Bayesian session uses the EI minimum improvement 0.01 (ei_xi) as its stopping reference, the plateau typically arrives within 20 to 40 rounds, and the convergence call is confirmed by human judgment; the machine spins on its own and humans look only at exceptions and end states. Iteration level: a variant review presents the quality report, parameter differences, and feedback records together, and the adjudication is logged. Evolution level: every jump of the test baseline—from 201 cases (2026-08-09) to 489 (2026-09-27) to the current ~1,027 (2026-10-25)—was accompanied by synchronized revisions of documents, thresholds, and process; a baseline update is an evolution-level event, not a daily change. Organizational output thereby becomes measurable: 19 modules, 154 endpoints, and ~1,027 tests are not an asset inventory but the sediment of six roles running on three rhythms over time.

  • Here = six roles own named deliverables, and rhythm unfolds at round, iteration, and evolution scales. In an LLM evaluation team = annotator, reviewer, and metric owner are separated; nobody both sets the questions and grades them.
  • Here = advice and adjudication are separated; the AI never signs the final state. In a general agent release flow = automation supplies evidence and a named human signs off.

Chapter 18 · Fast Variables and Order Parameters: The Seven Programs of Deep Evolution

Author: Quantum Swordsman (量子剑客) Volume VI · Evolution: letting the system grow by itself Core proposition: hold the order parameters firmly, let fast variables try and err, and use the closed loop to sediment experience into structure.

1. Introduction: why systems "get messier the more they are improved"

Many teams have lived through this: the system is visibly under continuous "optimization," yet the sense of direction is draining away. Last week a review said the front fascia should be sharper; this week the data says rounder scores higher, so the threshold quietly loosens. An architectural boundary set last month gets a breach opened for an "urgent requirement," and the breach never closes again. Every adjustment is defensible in isolation; stacked together, they are drift. The problem is not adjustment itself but the failure to distinguish two kinds of variables. Fast variables are adjustable step by step and respond quickly to disturbances; order parameters change slowly and govern the overall direction of the system's evolution. Changing an order parameter in the manner of a fast variable is the most common root of design losing control.

Synergetics tells us that a system's order is not achieved by controlling every variable but by holding the few order parameters firmly and letting the many fast variables relax freely within their frame. AI-native systems amplify the issue: a machine can produce thousands of fast-variable iterations overnight, and if the order parameters lack institutional protection, the direction of convergence is dragged around by noise and short-term scores. Using the platform's parameter space as the specimen, this chapter gives an engineering method for separating the two time scales, plus seven programs that keep a system in deep evolution.

Boundary statement. The seven guidelines and "order-parameter identification" are an evolutionary direction and engineering discipline [Design Principle]. What the codebase shows is the actual fixation of slow variables (parameter sets, quality thresholds, documents, evaluation baselines) and the actual tinkering of fast variables (candidate generation, experiment sessions). Automatic order-parameter identification is not implemented; humans designate it today from audits and metrics.

2. Principles

2.1 Separate the scales before optimizing anything

Every change proposal must first answer one question: does it modify a fast variable or an order parameter? Per-round parameter values, a single score fed back, a single defect fix—these are fast variables. Brand DNA, quality standards, design philosophy, architectural boundaries—these are order parameters. The two have entirely different change channels, approval levels, and record-keeping requirements; confusing them is losing control.

2.2 Fast variables optimize inside the order-parameter frame

Genes first, search second. The shape, boundaries, and frozen dimensions of a search space are themselves instantiations of the order parameters; all fast-variable exploration proceeds inside that frame. Trial and error is fully permitted within the frame—it is the fast variables' job. Outside the frame there is no trial and error, only trespass.

2.3 Changing an order parameter must be a knowledge-system event

Adjusting a quality threshold is not editing a number; it is a package event of updating the knowledge base, retraining or re-validating models, and revising the philosophy documents. Every order-parameter transition must follow the knowledge-layer process: a proposal, evidence, an adjudication, a version number. It must never be quietly rewritten by a single round's score or one wave of hot feedback.

2.4 Use the closed loop to sediment experience into structure

Deep evolution equals the continuous convergence of fast variables plus the controlled transition of order parameters, and the mechanism connecting the two is the closed loop: theory guides practice, practice produces data, data trains models, and feedback corrects theory. The endpoint of each circuit is not the deliverable but structural sediment—a clearer baseline, a sharper prior, a more explicit boundary.

3. Case study: two time scales and seven programs on the platform

The platform writes the dual time scales into the parameter space itself. The 22-dimensional CarParams are the fast variables' field of action; the 14-dimensional default search space of a Bayesian session, with its boundaries, is the order parameters' institutional constraint on them—dimensions carrying sizes, regulations, and structural hard points are frozen, and the acquisition function only proposes the next trial position inside the frame. The convergence criteria are layered as well: a single-round score is a fast-variable signal; the EI minimum improvement 0.01 (ei_xi) serves as the stopping reference, and the plateau—typically reached within 20 to 40 rounds and confirmed by session observation and human judgment—ends one round of fast variables; while the G1 5° and G2 2° quality thresholds move with no single-round result—their revision is an evolution-level event. Validation data shows the quality score converging steadily over observation rounds, which is the healthy shape of fast variables working inside a correct frame.

On this foundation, the platform's deep evolution follows seven programs, each with an explicit systems-science origin and an engineering landing point:

The seven programs share one structure: each combines an order-parameter action of "holding" with a fast-variable direction of "releasing." Hold the orchestration boundary, release algorithm iteration; hold the contracts, release module evolution; hold the threshold conventions, release trial and error inside the search space. Evolution is not a synonym for change but the coupling of controlled change with guarded constancy.
